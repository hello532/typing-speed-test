# Bank Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/bank-typing-test.html

Banking and clerical exams often include a typing round with a strict accuracy cut-off. Practise the format and the pace here.

Indian bank clerical recruitment commonly quotes 30 words per minute in English or 25 words per minute in Hindi, tested on a standard keyboard for a fixed period, with a small error allowance. Probationary officer and specialist officer posts generally do not include a typing test; clerical and some office-assistant posts do.

These figures appear in recruitment notifications rather than in a single national standard, so the notice for your cycle is the authority. Read it before you practise: the language, layout and error allowance all change between years.

Element | Typical form
Mode | Copy typing from a printed or on-screen passage
Language | English or Hindi, chosen at application
Duration | A fixed period, often around ten minutes
Scoring | Words per minute with an error allowance
Outcome | Qualifying - it does not add marks to the merit list

Because it is qualifying, the strategy is accuracy at a comfortable pace, not maximum speed. A clean 32 WPM passes; a 45 WPM run with 10% errors does not.

Use full-length rounds, not one-minute bursts. The error rate at minute eight is what decides the result, and it is invisible in a short round. Type passages with punctuation and capitals, since the actual passage includes them.

Add a Hindi round if you are taking the Hindi option, on the layout named in your notice - Krutidev, Remington or InScript are not interchangeable, and learning the wrong one creates conflicting reflexes.

Bank clerical requirements are close to SSC CHSL: both ask around 30-35 WPM and both are qualifying. The SSC CHSL page covers the 35/30 WPM and 8000 key-depression split in detail, and the government typing test page covers the general civil-service format.

## Bank Typing Test FAQ

### What typing speed do bank exams require?

Commonly 30 words per minute in English or 25 in Hindi for clerical posts, with a small error allowance. The recruitment notice for your cycle states the exact figure.

### Do bank PO exams include a typing test?

Generally no. Clerical and some office-assistant posts include a qualifying typing test; probationary officer and specialist officer posts usually do not.

### Is the bank typing test qualifying or merit?

Qualifying. It does not add marks to your total, which is why accuracy at a comfortable pace beats a fast, error-heavy run.

### Which language should I choose?

Whichever you type faster and more accurately. Hindi requires 25 WPM against 30 for English, but only if you are fluent in the layout your notice specifies.

### How long should I practise per session?

Full-length rounds of about ten minutes, three times a week, plus five minutes of accuracy work daily. Short bursts do not reveal the errors that appear late in a real test.
