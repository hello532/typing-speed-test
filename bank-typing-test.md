## Typing in banking exams

Many bank clerk and clerical recruitment exams include a typing test as a qualifying round: you must reach a set speed and accuracy, and failing it can end your application regardless of your written score. The format is a timed passage, usually in the language you applied in, with accuracy checked closely.

## The usual standard

Common requirements sit around 30 to 40 words per minute with at least 90% accuracy, and many boards set a hard floor on errors — a fixed number of mistakes is allowed and no more. Because the pass mark is about accuracy, clean typing beats raw speed.

## Passages and language

Bank typing rounds use ordinary prose, not technical jargon, but they are timed strictly and often use a fixed paragraph you cannot choose. Practise on uninterrupted passages of a few hundred words and get used to holding focus without fixing every tiny slip.

## How to practise

Set the timer to match your exam — often five minutes — and aim to finish comfortably under the error limit before you try to raise speed. Once you can pass at 97% accuracy, push your WPM up in small steps. Watch our data entry and government typing pages for the same family of clerical tests.

## Bank Typing Test FAQ

### What is a bank typing test?

It is a timed typing round used in many bank clerk and clerical recruitment exams, where you must reach a minimum speed and accuracy to qualify.

### What speed is required for bank typing tests?

Requirements vary by board, but most ask for around 30 to 40 words per minute with 90% or higher accuracy, often with a hard limit on allowed errors.

### Is accuracy or speed more important?

Accuracy. Banking typing rounds usually fail candidates who exceed the error limit, so a clean, correct passage matters more than a fast one.
