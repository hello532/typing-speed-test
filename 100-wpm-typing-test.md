# 100 WPM Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/100-wpm-typing-test.html

One hundred words per minute is roughly the 99th percentile of measured typists. It is not a faster version of 70 WPM — the technique is different, the practice is different, and for most jobs it is not required at all.

## What 100 WPM means numerically

One hundred words per minute is 500 characters per minute, about 8.3 characters per second, and 30,000 keystrokes per hour. In the CHI 2018 distribution with mean 51.56 and standard deviation 20.2, it sits near the 99th percentile — roughly one typist in a hundred.

For comparison: the fastest decile starts around 78 WPM, so 100 is not the edge of the distribution but well inside its tail. World-record competition typing exceeds 200 WPM on short bursts of known text, which is a different activity again.

## The technique difference

At 70 WPM you are typing words. At 100 WPM you are typing letter sequences with almost no conscious word recognition — the buffer is two to four words deep and the hands run ahead of attention. Typists at this level typically cannot tell you what they just typed.

That is why deliberate practice at 100 WPM looks strange: it is mostly very short maximum-speed bursts on familiar text, plus accuracy work on unfamiliar text, plus keyboard and layout optimisation. Long slow practice rounds stop helping.

## Is it worth it?

For employment, no. The highest clerical requirement you will meet is 75-80 WPM (legal, medical transcription) and court reporting uses a stenotype, not a keyboard. Above 80, extra speed adds no job options.

For work where you think in text — programming, live captioning, competitive typing, writing to a deadline — it removes the keyboard as a bottleneck entirely. That is a real but narrow benefit, and it costs months of practice to reach.

## If you decide to chase it

Keyboard: a mechanical board with short-travel switches is measurably faster for most people than a laptop chiclet keyboard. Try before buying.
Layout: some typists gain 5-10% from Colemak or Dvorak after a month of pain. Most do not; measure before committing.
Bursts: 15-second maximum rounds, ten per session, three sessions a week. Long rounds teach endurance you already have.
Accuracy gate: never accept below 97%. A 100 WPM score at 94% nets 94 and fails any real test.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 100 WPM rare?

Yes — roughly the 99th percentile of measured typists. About one in a hundred people in the CHI 2018 sample typed at or above that speed.

### How many keystrokes per hour is 100 WPM?

30,000 KPH. One hundred words per minute is 500 characters per minute, and 500 × 60 = 30,000.

### Do any jobs require 100 WPM?

No standard clerical role does. Legal and medical transcription top out around 80 WPM, and court reporting uses a stenotype at 225 WPM rather than a keyboard.

### How long does it take to reach 100 WPM?

Usually years from a standing start, and months from 80. The gains come from short maximum-speed bursts, hard-text accuracy work and keyboard optimisation rather than more of the same practice.

### Is 100 WPM possible with two fingers?

Effectively no. Two-finger typing plateaus around 35-40 WPM because each keystroke needs a visual target. Reaching 100 requires all ten fingers and no looking.
