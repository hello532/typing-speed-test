# Typing Test for 5th Graders

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/typing-test-for-5th-graders.html

By fifth grade most children can type 25–35 WPM if they have had regular practice, and accuracy matters far more than the number. Here is what the grade bands look like and how to practise without tears.

Classroom benchmarks vary by district and by how much keyboarding time a school actually provides, so treat any table as a rough band rather than a standard. The pattern that holds across published curricula is steady growth of roughly 5 WPM per grade once formal instruction begins.

Grade | Typical range | What to focus on
3rd | 10–20 WPM | Home row, not looking down
4th | 15–25 WPM | All letters, capitals with Shift
5th | 25–35 WPM | Accuracy above 90%, punctuation
6th–8th | 30–45 WPM | Sustained paragraphs, editing

A child who types 35 WPM by hunting for keys with two fingers has hit a ceiling that will cost years later. A child at 22 WPM with all ten fingers on the home row is on a curve that reaches 50 by secondary school with no extra lessons.

So score the round on errors, not words per minute. If accuracy is below 90%, the speed number is fiction — it is measuring how fast the child can produce text that has to be retyped.

Ten minutes, three times a week, is enough. One minute of warm-up, five minutes of the actual test, then two minutes of the child's own choice — quotes, pangrams, numbers. Stop while it is still fun; the failure mode is a forty-minute session that ends in an argument and no practice for a month.

Use the on-screen keyboard and finger guide on this site so the child can see which finger owns which key without an adult narrating it. Cover the hands with a light cloth for one round a week if looking down has become a habit — it is uncomfortable for two days and then it works.

Games are legitimate practice if the child is typing rather than watching. The test that matters: are the fingers moving on the home row, or is one finger stabbing at the keyboard while the other hand rests? If it is the second, the game is entertainment and the drill still has to happen separately.

A reasonable split for a fifth grader is one game session for every two test sessions. The test gives the number; the game gives the motivation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast should a 5th grader type?

Roughly 25–35 WPM with 90% or better accuracy if they have practised regularly. Bands vary by school and by how much keyboarding instruction the child has actually had.

### Is 20 WPM bad for a 5th grader?

No. It is normal for a child who has not had formal keyboarding instruction. What matters is whether the fingers are on the home row and whether accuracy is climbing.

### How long should practice be?

Ten minutes, three times a week. Short and frequent beats a long weekly session, and it keeps practice from becoming a punishment.

### Should my child look at the keyboard?

Not while testing. Looking down is the habit that caps speed. Cover the hands for one round a week so the child is forced to build a mental map of the keys.

### Do typing games help?

Yes when the child is genuinely typing with correct fingers. If the game rewards speed with two fingers, it teaches a habit that has to be undone later.
