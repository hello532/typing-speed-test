# SSC Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/ssc-typing-test.html

SSC typing rounds are a qualifying stage with a strict speed and accuracy bar. Practise the format here before the real thing.

## What the SSC typing test is

Several SSC recruitment exams include a typing test as a qualifying round, typically typing a passage in a set time to prove a minimum speed and accuracy. It is often held at a test centre and must be cleared to be considered for the post, no matter how well you did in the written exam.

## The usual requirement

SSC typing rounds commonly ask for around 35 words per minute in English, or roughly 30 words per minute in Hindi, with high accuracy. Some exams set the bar in a mix of speed and error count, so you must meet both to pass.

## Training for the format

The passage is delivered on screen and the clock does not stop, so the goal is steady, error-free typing rather than bursts of speed. Practise with light punctuation and everyday vocabulary — the passages are ordinary prose rather than technical text.

## Reaching the pass mark

Build your baseline with short daily sessions until you clear the required speed at 95%+ accuracy, then add a minute at a time to build stamina. Our government and bank typing pages cover the same clerical-test family; use them together for a rounded practice routine.

Most SSC-type government exams set a qualifying bar around 30–35 WPM (or the KPH equivalent) with a cap on mistakes, tested as a short timed passage. ## SSC Typing Test FAQ

### Is there a typing test in SSC exams?

Yes. Several SSC exams include a qualifying typing round where you must reach a minimum typing speed and accuracy, usually tested at a centre.

### What typing speed is needed for SSC?

Requirements vary by post and year, but roughly 35 words per minute in English or about 30 in Hindi is the commonly cited benchmark, with high accuracy.

### How can I practise for the SSC typing test?

Practise timed passages daily, keep accuracy above 95%, and build stamina for the full test length. Use a five-minute timer to mirror exam conditions.
