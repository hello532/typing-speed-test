# 60 WPM Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/60-wpm-typing-test.html

Sixty words per minute is the 66th percentile and the point where job descriptions stop saying 'typing required' and start saying 'fast, accurate typing'. It is also the floor for legal, medical and transcription roles.

Sixty words per minute is 300 characters per minute, five characters per second, 18,000 keystrokes per hour, and about the 66th percentile of measured typists. Two thirds of people are slower; one third are faster.

In employment terms it is a threshold: clerical adverts quote 40-50, whereas legal, medical and transcription adverts start at 60 and go up. Crossing 60 changes which jobs are open to you, which is why it is worth more effort than crossing 50.

At 60 WPM a 2% error rate means about one error every 8 seconds — roughly seven or eight errors in a five-minute test. On a net-scored test that costs you 5-6 WPM, dropping you back to the mid-50s. That is the arithmetic that keeps people at 55.

So the last stretch is not 'type faster'. It is 'hold 98% while typing at 60', which is a different drill: shorter rounds, stricter error budget, and stopping a round the moment accuracy falls below the floor rather than pushing to the timer.

30-second accuracy sprints: type at 90% of maximum for 30 seconds, stop, check errors. Repeat five times. This builds the accuracy-at-speed habit that longer rounds cannot.
Punctuation and capitals: most people lose 3-5 WPM here. Run rounds with quotes and sentence case deliberately until the Shift press stops breaking rhythm.
Numbers once a week: the number row is a separate motor skill and it is where a 60 WPM typist becomes a 52 WPM typist on real work.
One long round per week: ten minutes, to confirm the speed survives fatigue.

Medical transcription commonly asks 65-80 WPM with 98-99% accuracy, and it is measured over audio, which is harder than copying visible text. Court reporting is 225 WPM on a stenotype and is a different skill entirely.

For legal secretary work, 60-75 WPM is the advertised band, so 60 is the entry edge — aim for 65 before applying to a firm that tests.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 60 WPM fast?

It is about the 66th percentile and the point where job adverts start calling typing 'fast'. Top 10% is 78 WPM and above, so 60 is comfortably above average but not exceptional.

### What jobs require 60 WPM?

Legal secretary roles (60-75), transcription entry points (65+), medical admin and word-processing operator titles. Most general office roles stop at 50.

### How many keystrokes per hour is 60 WPM?

18,000 KPH. Sixty words per minute is 300 characters per minute, and 300 × 60 = 18,000.

### How long does it take to go from 50 to 60 WPM?

Four to eight weeks of short daily sessions for most people, provided the practice targets accuracy at speed rather than raw output.

### Does 60 WPM include errors?

Net scores subtract them. A 60 WPM gross score at 94% accuracy nets about 56, which is why the accuracy floor matters as much as the speed target.
