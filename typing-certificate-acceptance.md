# Will Employers Accept a Free Typing Certificate?

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/typing-certificate-acceptance.html

A free certificate printed from a website is self-reported evidence, not a credential. Some employers accept it, some require a proctored test, and knowing which is which saves you a wasted application - or a wasted fee.

Type | What it proves | Who accepts it
Self-issued certificate (printed from a website) | That you used the site and got a score | Some private employers, as a screening signal
Third-party timed result (dated, named, unproctored) | A specific score on a specific date | Many private employers; occasionally agencies
Proctored certificate (testing centre or remote proctor) | That someone observed the test | Government, courts, staffing agencies, some hospitals

The difference that matters is observation. A certificate nobody watched you earn establishes intent and baseline skill; it does not establish that the score is yours.

Private-sector applications where the recruiter just wants a rough number before scheduling an interview.
Freelance profiles - showing a dated result is better than claiming "fast typist".
Your own tracking - a monthly certificate is a simple progress record.

In all three cases the certificate is a convenience, not proof. Do not pay for one believing it changes the category.

Public-sector postings frequently state the requirement in a way that excludes self-issued documents: "must submit a typing test with your name and date", "proctored within the last twelve months", "issued by an approved testing centre". If the wording includes any of those, a website certificate is not the document they want.

Court and legal roles, dispatch centres and hospital transcription departments are the most consistent about requiring a proctored result. The cost is usually modest; the inconvenience is the appointment.

No stated proctoring. If nobody observes the test, you are buying a PDF.
No verification ID or lookup page. An employer cannot check it, so it carries no more weight than your own screenshot.
No expiry stated. Real credentials state validity; typing certificates typically run one to four years.
Scores that cannot be this high. A service guaranteeing 90+ WPM to anyone is selling paper.

Keep a dated, named screenshot of a five-minute result and put the number on your résumé with the test length and accuracy. Then let the employer test you - which most will, and which you will pass if the listed number is honest.

If a posting demands a proctored certificate, book one at a local testing centre or workforce office and treat the fee as part of the application cost. It is the only version that satisfies that wording.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do employers accept free online typing certificates?

Some private employers accept them as a screening signal, but they are self-reported. Government, court, dispatch and hospital roles usually require a proctored or employer-administered test instead.

### Is a typing certificate a real credential?

Only a proctored one. A certificate printed from a website shows that you took a test, not that anyone verified the result.

### How long is a typing certificate valid?

Typically one to four years depending on the issuer or agency. Postings often refuse results older than twelve months, so check the wording before reusing an old certificate.

### Can I put a certificate score on my résumé?

Yes, with the test length and accuracy, for example "55 WPM net, 98% accuracy, five-minute test". Expect to be re-tested, and list a score you can reproduce.

### Should I pay for a certificate?

Only when a posting asks for a proctored result or a specific issuer. For private-sector applications, a dated screenshot and an honest résumé line do the same job for free.
