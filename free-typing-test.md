## What a free typing test measures

Every round returns four numbers. Net WPM is words per minute after errors are deducted — the score employers and benchmarks quote. Raw WPM is your unadjusted finger speed, useful for seeing how much accuracy is costing you. Accuracy is correct keystrokes as a percentage of all keystrokes. CPM (characters per minute) is the same effort counted in keystrokes, which is how most Asian and some government tests score.

The gap between net and raw WPM is the single most useful signal on the result screen. A wide gap means speed is outrunning control; a narrow gap means you can safely push the pace. Read that gap before you read either score on its own.

## Typing tests from 15 seconds to 10 minutes

Length changes what you measure. A 30 second typing test is a snapshot of peak speed. A 2 minute typing test averages out a shaky start. The 3 minute and 5 minute typing test expose endurance, which is what pre-employment screening tends to check. The 10 minute typing test is the long-form version for transcription-style stamina.

Keep the length fixed when you track progress. Comparing a 30 second score with a 10 minute score tells you about endurance, not improvement.
