# Paragraph Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/paragraph-typing-test.html

Practise with full paragraphs instead of single words — the format real typing exams and job tests use.

A word-list test (the monkeytype style) shows one word at a time and rewards raw reaction. A paragraph test shows continuous prose, so it also measures reading ahead, punctuation, capitals, and the ability to hold a rhythm across sentences.

Employers test with paragraphs because that is what the job looks like: continuous text with full stops, names, commas and the occasional number. A word-list score is a training metric; a paragraph score is a work metric.

Three reasons. Punctuation and capitals add keystrokes that a word list omits. Sentence structure means the next word is predictable, but only if you read ahead - which most typists do not. And length means fatigue enters: errors in the fourth minute are usually attention, not technique.

The result is a score typically 5-15% lower than the same typist's word-list number, and 5-10 WPM lower than a one-minute burst on the same text.

Length | What it measures | Use it when
1 minute | Burst speed | Daily warm-up, quick comparison
3 minutes | Common screening length | Interview practice, exam rehearsal
5 minutes | Working pace | The number you quote; matches most job standards
10 minutes | Endurance | Transcription, data entry, long document work

Start with one minute to warm up, then run a five-minute round and record net WPM plus accuracy. If accuracy is under 96%, stop chasing speed and run accuracy rounds for a week - it is the cheaper fix.

For exam-style practice, use the same length as the exam and include punctuation. For a written job test, add one round of the quotes test, which is the closest thing to real document text. A paragraph score is only useful if you can reproduce it on a second attempt, at a different time of day, on a keyboard you have not warmed up on.

## Paragraph Typing Test FAQ

### What is a paragraph typing test?

A test using continuous prose rather than a word list, so it measures reading ahead, punctuation and capitals alongside raw speed.

### Why is my paragraph speed lower than my word-list speed?

Punctuation and capitals add keystrokes, sentence structure requires reading ahead, and length introduces fatigue. A 5-15% lower score is normal.

### How long should the paragraph be?

Five minutes for a number you can quote, three minutes to match most screening tests, ten minutes for transcription and data-entry work.

### Do employers use paragraphs or word lists?

Paragraphs, because that is what the work looks like. Word-list scores are useful for training but they are not what screening tools measure.

### Is this the same as a passage typing test?

Yes. Paragraph, passage and prose tests all mean continuous text; the differences are length and difficulty, not format.
