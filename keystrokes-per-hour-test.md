# Keystrokes Per Hour Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/keystrokes-per-hour-test.html

A keystrokes per hour (KPH) test measures typing speed the way many data entry employers do &mdash; in keystrokes over an hour, not words per minute. Data entry roles commonly require 8,000&ndash;10,000 KPH. Type the passage below and convert your result to KPH instantly.

## What is keystrokes per hour?

Keystrokes per hour (KPH or KSPH) counts how many individual key presses you make in an hour, counting every character including spaces. It is the standard measure for data entry and numeric keypad work because it does not depend on how long the words happen to be. A fast data entry operator reaches 10,000-15,000 KPH.

If you know your characters per minute (CPM), multiply by 60 to get KPH: a steady 160 CPM equals 9,600 KPH. This test shows your characters per minute live, so you can read your KPH straight off the result.

## KPH vs WPM vs CPM

All three describe the same typing, just at different scales. WPM counts words (5 characters each) per minute; CPM counts characters per minute; KPH counts keystrokes per hour. Because a &lsquo;word&rsquo; is standardised as 5 characters, the conversions are fixed:

Your speed | WPM | CPM | KPH
Average | 40 | 200 | 12,000
Good | 60 | 300 | 18,000
Fast | 80 | 400 | 24,000
Elite | 100 | 500 | 30,000

The table assumes no error penalty; employers usually apply a net-accuracy penalty, so your effective KPH is lower than the raw figure.

Job postings for data entry and clerical work often ask for 8,000-10,000 KPH with a high accuracy rate; some set the bar at 12,000 KPH. ## How do KPH requirements convert to WPM?

Divide by 300: WPM = KPH ÷ 300, so a 10,000 KPH posting is asking for about 33 WPM. For the complete table of KPH, CPM and WPM requirements by job and exam, see typing speed requirements by job and exam.

## Sources for the numbers on this page

Speed and accuracy figures quoted here follow the largest published study of everyday typing: Dhakal et al., CHI 2018, Observations on Typing from 136 Million Keystrokes, which measured 136,857,600 keystrokes from 168,960 volunteers. It reports a mean of 51.56 WPM (SD 20.2), a mean uncorrected error rate of 1.17%, and a mean keystrokes-per-character rate of 1.173. Those are the numbers this page's bands are checked against.

The claim that finger consistency and keypress preparation predict speed — more than which finger presses which key — comes from Feit, Weir and Oulasvirta, CHI 2016, a motion-capture study of 36 typists. The two studies disagree on how much formal touch-typing training matters, and this page says so where it matters rather than picking the tidier result.

Where a figure is an estimate rather than a measurement — job requirements, age bands, exam cut-offs — it is drawn from the ranges employers and exam boards publish and is labelled as an estimate. Nothing on this page is a benchmark we ran ourselves.

## Keystrokes, characters and words are three units

Numeric keypad work is usually scored in keystrokes per hour, but the same minute of typing can be read three ways: keystrokes per hour, characters per minute, and the five-character words per minute used by general typing tests. Because digits and operators are single characters, ten key speeds look far higher in KPH than the same effort looks in WPM - convert before comparing yourself with anyone quoting a different unit.

## Keystrokes per hour FAQ

### What is a good keystrokes per hour score?

For data entry work, 8,000 to 10,000 KPH is the usual requirement, and fast operators reach 12,000 to 15,000 KPH with 99% accuracy. General office roles often accept lower KPH because typing is only part of the job.

### How do I convert KPH to WPM?

WPM = KPH ÷ 300. Because 1 word is 5 characters and there are 60 minutes in an hour, 60 WPM equals 18,000 KPH.

### How do I convert CPM to KPH?

Multiply characters per minute by 60: 160 CPM equals 9,600 KPH.

### Does this test score accuracy too?

Yes. It reports your characters per minute live and, like every test on this site, gives your accuracy and error count after the run, so you can judge net rather than gross speed.

### Is KPH used for 10 key tests?

Yes. Numeric keypad (10 key) tests are almost always scored in keystrokes per hour, which is why data entry applicants are often asked for a 10 key KPH figure.
