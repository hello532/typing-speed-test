# Typing Test for Teachers

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/typing-test-for-teachers.html

Teachers type constantly — lesson plans, reports, marking, emails. See where your speed sits against what the job demands.

## Why typing matters for teachers

Teaching involves a constant stream of typed work: lesson plans, schemes of work, reports, feedback and parent emails. Being a comfortable typist saves hours every week and leaves more energy for the classroom.

## What speed is expected

Teaching itself rarely sets a formal typing test, but the admin side rewards 45 WPM or more with good accuracy. Office-based education roles and school administrators often expect the same 40 to 55 WPM range as other admin jobs, and support-staff interviews may include a short timed test.

## Tools teachers use

Mark and feedback, online portals and shared documents all reward fast, accurate typing. Keeping accuracy high means fewer corrections in reports that others will read, which matters as much as speed.

## Practise in small gaps

You do not need a big block of time. A one-minute test between lessons, repeated daily, is enough to lift your speed steadily. Watch the accuracy figure and aim to hold it above 96% as your WPM rises.

## Typing Test for Teachers FAQ

### Do teachers need a typing test?

Usually not to teach, but office-based education roles and support-staff interviews may include a short timed typing test like other administrative jobs.

### What typing speed should a teacher have?

Around 45 words per minute or more with good accuracy is comfortable for the heavy admin load of teaching. Administrative roles often expect 40 to 55 WPM.

### How can busy teachers practise?

Use short one-minute tests in spare moments during the day. Daily short sessions raise speed faster than occasional long ones.
