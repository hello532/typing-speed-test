## words per minute test

A words per minute test (WPM test) measures how many words you can type in one minute. The rule is simple: every five characters counts as one word, so longer words add more to your score. Take the test, type the text shown on screen, and your WPM, accuracy and error count are calculated live as you type.

In practice a typing words per minute test is the same measurement under a different name: you type for a fixed time, the test counts correct characters, and dividing that count by five and by the elapsed minutes gives your WPM. Nothing else changes the number, so any result you see here is directly comparable with other typing tests. The name changes with word order too — WPM typing test, typing test WPM and words per minute test all point at the same measurement, so whichever phrasing you searched for, this page scores it the same way.

## What is a good WPM score?

What counts as fast depends on context, but the bands below hold for most desk work. Around 40 WPM is typical, 60 WPM handles office and writing work comfortably, and 80+ WPM is genuinely fast — professionals land between 90 and 120. New typists should chase 95%+ accuracy first; speed follows on its own.

## WPM vs CPM

CPM means characters per minute. Some sites show CPM instead of WPM. Because one word equals five characters, you can convert easily: WPM = CPM / 5. This test shows you both numbers on the result panel so you can compare with any other typing test.

Prefer characters per minute? Try the CPM typing test, where CPM = WPM &times; 5.

## Typing words per minute test: what the number means

A typing words per minute test reports one number for one minute of typing, using a convention rather than a literal word count: every five characters typed counts as one word, whether those characters formed short words, long words, numbers or punctuation. The Wikipedia entry for words per minute documents the same five-character standard and the reference bands that most tests still quote: professional typists sit around 43 to 80 WPM, and advanced typists go above 120 WPM.

Two numbers come out of a typing words per minute test and they are not interchangeable. Gross (raw) WPM counts every keystroke, including the ones you had to correct. Net WPM deducts those errors, which is why a fast, careless run can post a high raw score and still be the slower run. When a job application or a certificate asks for your speed, the figure they want is net WPM with accuracy reported next to it - anything under about 95 percent accuracy means the speed is not yet usable in real work.

If your target is quoted in keystrokes rather than words - common for data entry and for exam scoring - measure it directly with the CPM typing test, which reports characters per minute and keystrokes per hour from the same round.

## Typing test FAQ

### What does a words-per-minute test measure?

It converts everything you type into a WPM score using the 5-characters-per-word standard, alongside accuracy and error counts. This page runs the test at 60 seconds, the standard length employers quote.

### How is WPM calculated?

WPM = (correct characters ÷ 5) ÷ minutes elapsed. The industry standard treats every 5 characters as one word, so longer words count more. Raw WPM ignores errors, while net WPM subtracts them.

### How long should a WPM test run?

Sixty seconds is the accepted standard for a reportable score. Shorter sprints flatter you; longer tests (3 to 5 minutes) are steadier. Retake and average three rounds for a number you can quote.

### How do I raise my WPM score?

Push accuracy above 95% first — errors hurt net WPM twice, once in speed and once in the penalty. Then build speed with daily practice rounds and retest weekly.

### What is a typing words per minute test?

It is a timed typing test that reports your speed in words per minute. You type for 60 seconds (or another length), the test divides your correct characters by five to get words, then divides by the elapsed minutes. A 300-character round is 60 WPM.

### How many words per minute should I type?

Most adults land between 40 and 60 WPM. Office and writing roles usually ask for 60 to 80, data entry and transcription posts 80-plus, and 90 to 120 is professional territory. Anything under 30 WPM is a beginner still building touch typing foundations. Check the average typing speed bands before judging a single round.

### What is a typing words per minute test?

A typing words per minute test measures how many five-character words you type correctly in sixty seconds. Every five keystrokes count as one word regardless of real word length, so the score stays comparable across different passages. Net WPM deducts errors and is the figure to quote; raw WPM ignores them.
