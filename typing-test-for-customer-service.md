# Typing Test for Customer Service

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/typing-test-for-customer-service.html

Written support is a typing job with a customer attached. Chat and email roles usually ask 35–50 WPM, and the thing that gets measured in probation is not speed but whether your replies are clear and typo-free.

## Speed by channel

Channel | Typical WPM | What the job really tests
Phone support with notes | 30-40 | Capturing names, references and next steps while listening
Email support | 40-50 | Clear, correct written English; template editing under time pressure
Live chat | 45-60 | Typing the conversation itself, often with two or three chats open
Social media support | 40-55 | Public tone, short replies, high visibility of mistakes

The bands come from advertised requirements and from what contact-centre training programmes assume. A posting that quotes 35 WPM for email is unusual; 45 is the common floor for written channels.

## Why accuracy outranks speed here

A typo in a reply is read by a customer who is already frustrated. It costs trust in a way that a slow reply does not. Support teams therefore judge written output on clarity and correctness, and they will happily keep a 45 WPM agent who writes cleanly over a 65 WPM agent who needs every message checked.

Practise accordingly: type a short reply, then proofread it. Two-thirds of the improvement in the first month comes from the proofreading habit, not from the keyboard.

## Drills that match the work

Two-minute sentence rounds for the base pace, one a day.
Template editing - take a canned reply and rewrite the middle sentence for a specific customer in under 30 seconds. That is the actual task.
Numbers mode once a week for order and reference numbers, which is where written support makes its costly mistakes.
Two conversations at once - type alternate replies to two prompts without mixing them up. Chat roles test this informally within the first week.

## Frequently asked questions

### What WPM do customer service jobs require?

Usually 35-50 WPM depending on channel: 30-40 for phone support notes, 40-50 for email, and 45-60 for live chat where you type the conversation itself.

### Is 40 WPM enough for live chat?

It is marginal. Chat roles usually ask 45-60 because you type the entire interaction, often across two or three concurrent conversations.

### Does accuracy matter more than speed?

Yes in written support. A typo in a reply reaches a frustrated customer and costs trust; a slightly slower correct reply does not. Teams keep clean 45 WPM agents over sloppy 65 WPM ones.

### How do I practise typing while handling a customer?

Practise template editing: rewrite the middle sentence of a canned reply for a specific customer in under 30 seconds, then check it for typos. That is the real task.

### Are there tests for chat support roles?

Often yes, usually a short typing test plus a written role-play. The role-play is the deciding part, which is why tone and clarity practice matters as much as WPM.
