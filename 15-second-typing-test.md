# 15 Second Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/15-second-typing-test.html

A 15 second typing test measures burst speed, not endurance. Fifteen seconds is roughly 10 to 22 words. Use it to check warm-up and compare against your 1 minute score.

The 15 second typing test is the shortest timed round on this site. Fifteen seconds is enough to type roughly 10 to 22 words depending on your speed, which gives a readable burst number without the fatigue that creeps into a five or ten minute round.

For a steadier figure that averages out a good or bad sentence, use the 30 second typing test or the 1 minute typing test.

It tells you your ceiling, not your average. Short rounds reward a fast start and are less affected by pacing mistakes, so the number is usually higher than a longer test taken the same day.

The useful signal is the difference between this and your one minute score. Within about 20 percent means your speed is stable; wider than that means you are starting fast and fading, which is a pacing problem rather than a speed one.

Use it to check a warm-up, to test a new keyboard or layout quickly, or as a daily pulse check when you do not have a full minute. Do not use it as your headline WPM: employers and typing certificates quote one minute or longer, so that is the number to compare against requirements.

For how a short burst compares with the standard used in job tests, see typing speed requirements.

## 15 second typing test FAQ

### How many words is 15 seconds of typing?

At 40 WPM about 10 words, at 60 WPM about 15 and at 90 WPM about 22. The exact count depends on word length in the passage.

### Is a 15 second test accurate?

It measures burst speed well but is easier to skew than a longer round, because one fast or slow sentence moves the average more. Treat it as a ceiling, not your headline WPM.

### Why is my 15 second score higher than my 1 minute score?

Short rounds reward a fast start and carry less fatigue. A gap wider than roughly 20 percent usually means pacing, not raw speed.
