## colemak typing test

Most typing tests only draw a QWERTY keyboard, which is useless for Colemak typing practice: the label you are hunting for is not on the key you press. This test flips the on-screen keyboard to the Colemak layout, keeping the physical QWERTY key positions and relabelling every key, so the finger guide on screen matches exactly what a Colemak typist sees.

Nothing is installed and nothing changes your system. The page only draws the Colemak keyboard; you switch your operating system input to Colemak, start typing, and the test scores the characters your layout produces - so your WPM, accuracy, consistency and the per-key heatmap are all measured for real Colemak typing.

## Why Colemak

Colemak was published in 2006 as a middle path between QWERTY and Dvorak. It keeps the common shortcut keys Z, X, C and V in their QWERTY places and changes only 17 keys, so the switch is far gentler than Dvorak while still putting the most-used letters under your fingers.

On the same English passage, Colemak keeps about 73% of letters on the home row (QWERTY is near 36%) and cuts repeated same-finger presses to roughly 1.5%, versus about 7.9% on QWERTY. Less finger travel usually means more comfort over long sessions; it does not guarantee a higher top speed, which still depends mostly on practice.

## How to practice Colemak typing

Set your operating system input to Colemak first, otherwise you would be typing QWERTY characters while looking at a Colemak keyboard. Start with short two-to-five minute sessions and resist looking at your hands - the finger guide and on-screen keyboard exist to replace that habit.

Expect your speed to drop well below your QWERTY speed for the first week or two; that is the switch cost, not a verdict on Colemak. Track accuracy here before speed, because accuracy recovers first and speed follows it. Compare Colemak with Dvorak and QWERTY on the keyboard layouts page.

## Colemak typing FAQ

### Does this test switch my keyboard to Colemak?

No. The test only shows a Colemak keyboard on this page. You switch your own operating system input to Colemak in its keyboard or language settings, and the test then scores whatever characters your layout produces.

### Do I need an account or to install anything?

No. The Colemak keyboard is drawn by the page itself, the test runs entirely in your browser, and your results are stored only in this browser.

### Is Colemak easier to learn than Dvorak?

For most QWERTY typists, yes. Colemak changes only 17 keys and keeps Z, X, C and V in their QWERTY positions, so common shortcuts and much of your muscle memory still apply. Dvorak moves almost every key.

### Will my Colemak WPM be lower than my QWERTY WPM at first?

Yes, usually much lower for the first week or two while you rebuild muscle memory. Measure accuracy first; once accuracy returns to your normal level, speed follows.

### Which keys does Colemak move?

Colemak keeps the bottom row's Z X C V B and the Q W A positions, and remaps the rest so E, T, N, S, I, R and O land on the home row. The full relabelling is drawn on the on-screen keyboard above.

### Can I switch back to QWERTY or try Dvorak here?

Yes. The keyboard toolbar has a QWERTY / Dvorak / Colemak switch and your choice is remembered in this browser, so the same page works for any of the three layouts.
