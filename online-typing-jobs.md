# Online Typing Jobs

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/online-typing-jobs.html

Online typing jobs are real work with real requirements — mostly speed, accuracy and reliability. Here is what the roles actually involve, what they measure, and how to avoid the scams that surround them.

## The four main types of online typing jobs

Data entry is the largest category: transferring information into forms, spreadsheets and databases, usually measured in KPH — keystrokes per hour — with 8,000–12,000 KPH (about 27–40 WPM) as the common requirement band and a hard accuracy cutoff around 98%. Transcription converts audio to text and pays for sustained accuracy at 300+ CPM (60+ WPM): general transcription needs no special background, while legal and medical work pays more and demands terminology training.

Captioning and subtitling sits between the two: real-time listening plus clean formatting. Chat and email support is the fourth category — typed customer service where 40–60 WPM with excellent written accuracy is the baseline. Every one of these roles is trained on the same foundation: a verified speed score and a near-error-free record.

## What employers and platforms actually measure

Expect three measurements before any paid work: a timed typing test (usually one to three minutes, scored as net WPM with an accuracy gate), an accuracy or proofreading exercise, and a small paid-or-unpaid trial task. Nobody hires on self-reported speed alone, so having a recent, verifiable score matters more than the number on your résumé.

## How to spot typing job scams

The rule that eliminates nearly every scam: legitimate typing work never asks you to pay. No registration fee, no training fee, no 'software package' to buy, no refundable deposit. Any offer that reverses the money flow is not a job.

Secondary red flags: pay rates wildly above market for easy work, vague company identity, and interviews conducted entirely over chat apps. Real platforms test you, pay on a schedule, and never need your money. When in doubt, search the company name plus 'scam' before sharing any personal information.

## How to qualify and get started

The practical path: first, verify your speed — take a data entry typing test or the KPH test and record your score. Second, close the gap if you are below the band for your target role; two to four weeks of daily practice moves most people 10+ WPM. Third, assemble proof: a recent test result and, for some roles, a typing certificate you can attach to applications.

Then apply widely and treat the first month as paid training: accuracy ratings on early tasks unlock better-paying work on every platform. Reliability compounds faster than speed.

## Captioning and subtitling: the fourth lane

Captioning turns video audio into timed text — part transcription, part formatting discipline. The typing requirement sits around 60 WPM with excellent accuracy, but the real skill is timing: syncing text to speech within style rules for line length and reading speed. Subtitling adds translation-free condensing: saying the same thing in fewer characters so viewers can keep up.

Entry routes mirror transcription: platform tests with a style guide, a short trial, then paid work. It is a good fit for typists who are accurate and detail-oriented but find long-form transcription monotonous.

## Chat and email support: typing as customer service

Support roles rarely say 'typing job' in the title, yet typing is the job: simultaneous chats, templated-but-personalised replies, and ticket summaries all day. The benchmark is 40–60 WPM with near-perfect written accuracy, because a public typo to a customer is worse than a slow reply.

These roles are the most beginner-friendly entry into paid typing work: training is provided, scripts exist, and accuracy metrics are visible from day one. Many people use a year of support work as the springboard into transcription or data-specialist roles.

## Freelance platforms vs direct employment

Gig and freelance platforms offer fast entry and flexible hours, with pay per task and heavy early competition; your rating history is everything. Direct employment — staffing agencies and employer career pages — offers steadier hours and benefits, with formal tests and interviews as the gate.

Most working typists blend the two: a part-time employed role for stability, platform work for extra income. Start wherever the barrier is lowest for you; the proof of speed and accuracy you build transfers between both worlds unchanged.

## Equipment and environment that protect your rating

Ratings in online typing work die from two killers: errors and missed deadlines. A comfortable full-size keyboard, a quiet workspace, and a second monitor or split screen for source material reduce both. Backup internet — even a phone hotspot — saves a deadline when the connection drops mid-task.

Track your own metrics from day one: tasks completed, error rate, average time per task. Platforms show you some of this; keeping your own log lets you spot which task types actually pay best for your speed profile, and double down on them.

## A realistic first-90-days roadmap

Days 1–14: verify your score, pick your target role from the table in this article, and close any speed gap with daily practice. Days 15–30: assemble proof (recent test, certificate), create profiles on two platforms, and take every entry test available — they are free practice even where you do not pass.

Days 31–90: protect your accuracy rating like income, because it is. Accept lower-paying tasks only while they build ratings, specialise in the task type your log says pays you best, and re-test your typing monthly to keep proof current. The typists earning steadily at day 90 are not the fastest; they are the most reliable.

## Online Typing Jobs FAQ

### Are online typing jobs legitimate?

Yes — data entry, transcription, captioning and chat support are real categories. The rule for spotting scams: legitimate typing work never asks you to pay any fee. Money flows to you, never from you.

### What typing speed do online jobs require?

Data entry typically 8,000–12,000 KPH (27–40 WPM) with ~98% accuracy; transcription 300+ CPM (60+ WPM); chat support 40–60 WPM. Every platform tests you — self-reported speed is never enough.

### Do I need experience for online typing jobs?

Entry-level data entry and chat support need only a passing test score. Transcription benefits from practice but general transcription is open to beginners; legal and medical variants want terminology training.

### How do I prove my typing speed to employers?

With a recent verifiable test result. Take a timed test, save the result, and attach a typing certificate where applications allow it. Test your score now on the data entry typing test.
