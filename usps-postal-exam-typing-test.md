# USPS Postal Exam Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/usps-postal-exam-typing-test.html

The honest answer first: the USPS virtual entry assessments do not contain a typing test. They measure work simulation, memory and customer service judgement. Keyboard speed still matters on the job, and some roles test it locally.

## What the postal assessments actually contain

USPS replaced its older paper exams with virtual entry assessments tailored to job families: 474 for mail handler, 475 for mail processing, 476 for customer service and 477 for maintenance. They present work-sample scenarios - sorting decisions, address matching, register and money-order arithmetic, customer interactions - and score judgement and speed of decision rather than keystrokes.

That is why a candidate searching for a postal typing test usually finds practice sites selling a typing drill that does not match the exam. Practising WPM will not raise a 476 score; practising the scenario format will.

## Where keyboard speed does matter

Data-entry-adjacent roles - address updates, redirects and claims work move faster with clean numeric typing.
Facility-level checks - some offices still administer a basic keyboarding or data-entry exercise during onboarding.
The job itself - retail counter and processing roles type addresses, tracking numbers and short notes all day, and an unfamiliar keyboard slows that down.

The figure that appears in comparable clerical settings is 30-40 WPM with accuracy above 95%. Treat it as a sensible personal target, not a published USPS requirement.

## What to practise instead, if the exam is your goal

Work on the scenario skills: reading an address quickly and spotting the mismatch, doing change-making arithmetic without a calculator, remembering a short instruction sequence, and choosing the answer that follows procedure rather than the one that feels kindest.

Then use typing practice as a background habit - five minutes a day for the job, not for the exam. The two goals are different and mixing them wastes the preparation window.

## If you do want a keyboard number

Take a five-minute sentence test and a two-minute numbers test, record net WPM and accuracy for both, and keep the screenshot. It is useful for the interview conversation and for any local keyboarding check, and it costs five minutes.

For address-style content specifically, the alphanumeric typing test is closer to the work than a prose passage, because postcodes and route numbers are mixed letter-digit strings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the USPS exam include a typing test?

No. The virtual entry assessments (474, 475, 476, 477) are scenario-based work simulations. They do not report a words-per-minute score.

### What typing speed does USPS require?

There is no published universal WPM requirement for the entry assessments. Comparable clerical roles quote 30-40 WPM with 95% accuracy, which is a sensible target for the job rather than an exam rule.

### Why do websites sell postal typing practice?

Because people search for it. The practice is usually generic typing drill content that does not match the assessment format, so it will not raise a 474-477 score.

### What should I practise for the postal exam?

Address comparison, money and change arithmetic, short instruction memory, and procedure-following judgement. Those are the four skills the scenarios actually test.

### Is keyboarding tested at the facility?

Sometimes. Some offices run a basic keyboarding or data-entry exercise during onboarding. It is usually a low bar, but a clean 40 WPM with accurate digits removes the risk.
