# Bigram Typing Practice

A bigram typing practice drill targeting the most common English letter combinations.

## What is bigram typing practice?

A bigram is a pair of consecutive letters that appears very often in English: th, he, in, er, on, ed, nd, ti, es, re, ou, ar and st. These thirteen pairs dominate English text, so a bigram typing practice drill that repeats them builds the exact finger coordination that real-world typing demands.

When the letters th appear in a word, your two fingers must coordinate - one strikes t while the other prepares h. If this transition is hesitant, every word containing th is slowed. Drill the bigram and every matching word speeds up.

## Common vs rare bigrams

There are two drill modes. Common bigrams (th, he, in, er, on) train the transitions you use every minute. Rare bigrams (wh, kn, gh, qu, ch, ght, ugh, ought) train the combinations that break rhythm even in skilled typists. Start with common until they feel automatic, then switch to rare for a challenge.

Alternating between the two keeps each round fresh while covering the full range of English letter combinations.

## How to use this page

Take a 15-second round on common bigrams, note your weak-key report, then a 15-second round on rare bigrams. The keys in both reports are your true weak keys. Drill them until they stop appearing, then retest with the 1 minute typing test.

## Bigram practice for exam preparation

Government and clerk typing tests in India test accuracy and speed on timed passages. Bigram fluency directly improves passage typing because common letter combinations no longer cause hesitation. Practice common bigrams until automatic, then hard bigrams until comfortable, and finally full sentences to tie them together. Punctuation symbols carry more CPM weight than people expect, because they break letter rhythm: practise them with quotes mode or the punctuation test on this site to keep every keystroke type at the same automatic level.
