# 10 Key Typing Test

A 10 key typing test measures how fast you type on the numeric keypad &mdash; the number pad on the right of a full-size keyboard &mdash; using the fingers of your right hand. It is the standard speed test for data entry, accounting and finance work. Take the timed number-pad test below and get your speed instantly.

## What is a 10 key typing test?

10 key typing (also written ten key typing or 10-key) is touch typing on the numeric keypad: the number, arithmetic and Enter keys on the right of a full-size keyboard. Instead of hunting for digits across the top row, a 10 key typist keeps the right hand anchored on 4 5 6 with the index, middle and ring fingers, and reaches every other key from there. It is the skill behind fast data entry, bookkeeping and banking work.

This page runs a numpad typing test in numbers mode, so the practice text is digits, decimals, dates and amounts - exactly the characters a 10 key operator types all day. Switch the Time chips to 15 or 30 seconds for a quick check, or run 3-5 minutes for a stable result.

## The 10 key home row (4 5 6)

Rest your index finger on 4, your middle finger on 5 and your ring finger on 6 - these three usually have a raised bump or a dot so you can find them without looking. The index finger also covers 1 and 7, the middle covers 2 and 8, the ring covers 3 and 9, and the little finger handles 0, . and Enter. Your thumb rests on the 0 key on many layouts.

The golden rule of ten key typing is the same as touch typing on the letter keys: eyes on the screen, fingers always returning to 4 5 6 between digits. Speed comes from never looking down.

## How is 10 key speed measured?

10 key speed is usually quoted in keystrokes per hour (KPH) or characters per minute (CPM), not words per minute. A comfortable 10 key speed for data entry is about 8,000-10,000 KPH (roughly 130-165 CPM), and strong operators reach 12,000-15,000 KPH with 99%+ accuracy.

Level | 10 key speed
Beginner | 3,000-5,000 KPH
Average | 5,000-8,000 KPH
Data entry ready | 8,000-10,000 KPH
Fast / professional | 12,000-15,000+ KPH

## Why practise the number pad separately?

The top number row and the numeric keypad use completely different muscle memory. Typing letters fast does not make you fast at the number pad, and many people lose a data entry job interview on the 10 key portion alone. Practising the numpad on its own trains the right hand to hit 0-9 and the decimal point without a single glance at the keyboard.

If you need both, warm up with this 10 key typing test and then check your general speed on the number typing test or the main typing speed test.

## Keystrokes, characters and words are three units

Numeric keypad work is usually scored in keystrokes per hour, but the same minute of typing can be read three ways: keystrokes per hour, characters per minute, and the five-character words per minute used by general typing tests. Because digits and operators are single characters, ten key speeds look far higher in KPH than the same effort looks in WPM - convert before comparing yourself with anyone quoting a different unit.

## 10 key typing FAQ

### What is a 10 key typing test?

A 10 key typing test measures how quickly you type on the numeric keypad (the number pad on the right of a full-size keyboard) using your right hand, keeping the fingers anchored on the 4, 5 and 6 keys.

### What is a good 10 key speed?

For data entry work, aim for at least 8,000 keystrokes per hour (about 133 characters per minute). Experienced operators reach 12,000 to 15,000 KPH with 99% accuracy or better.

### Is 10 key the same as touch typing?

It is touch typing applied to the numeric keypad. You keep your eyes on the screen, rest the fingers on 4 5 6, and let each finger own its column of digits.

### How do I improve my 10 key speed?

Practise a few minutes a day with your eyes off the keyboard, keep the index, middle and ring fingers on 4, 5 and 6, and prioritise accuracy &mdash; errors cost more time than a slightly slower but clean run.

### Does this work on a laptop without a number pad?

On laptops with no dedicated keypad you can use an external USB numeric keypad, or practise the digits with the number-typing mode instead, which uses the top number row.
