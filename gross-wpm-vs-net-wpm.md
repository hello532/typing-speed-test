# Gross WPM vs Net WPM

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/gross-wpm-vs-net-wpm.html

Two typing tests can score the same run ten words apart. The difference is gross (all characters typed) versus net (errors subtracted). Here are both formulas and one worked example.

## The two formulas

Gross WPM: every character you produced, including wrong ones, divided by five and by the minutes elapsed. Net WPM: the same calculation after the incorrect characters are removed. Some tests subtract a fixed penalty per error instead of the characters themselves; both arrive at a lower number, they just describe the penalty differently.

Method | Formula | What it rewards
Gross (raw) WPM | characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes | Speed alone
Net WPM | (characters − errors) ÷ 5 ÷ minutes | Speed that survives accuracy
Adjusted WPM (AWPM) | all characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes, errors counted as typed | School and exam scoring, where every keystroke is credited

## One run, three scores

Suppose you type 300 characters in 60 seconds and 12 of them are wrong. Gross is 300 ÷ 5 ÷ 1 = 60 WPM. Net is (300 − 12) ÷ 5 ÷ 1 = 57.6 WPM. Adjusted WPM stays at 60, because the method credits what you typed and reports accuracy separately.

That is a 2.4 WPM spread on a single minute at 96% accuracy. At 90% accuracy the spread widens to 6 WPM, and at 85% it is 9 WPM — which is why the same typist can look fast on one site and average on another.

## Which score belongs on a CV

Quote net WPM, and quote the accuracy beside it: "62 WPM net, 98.5% accuracy, 5-minute test". Anyone who screens typists knows the difference between the methods, and an unqualified number invites the assumption that you picked the flattering one.

If a job ad specifies a method — some government and court tests do — report that method and say so. Never mix: a gross 70 next to someone's net 65 is not a like-for-like comparison, and it makes the higher number look inflated.

## Why longer tests score lower

Gross and net both fall as the clock runs, because the first thirty seconds are warm-up pace you cannot hold. A 15-second score is a burst; a 10-minute score is a shift. When you compare yourself to a published average, check how long the test was before you conclude anything about your speed.

The practical rule: train on one-minute rounds, verify on five, and quote the five-minute number. It is the only one an employer can reproduce.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between gross and net WPM?

Gross WPM counts every character you typed, right or wrong. Net WPM subtracts the incorrect characters first, so it reflects usable output. Net is always equal to or lower than gross.

### How do I calculate net WPM?

Take the characters typed, subtract the errors, divide by five, then divide by the minutes elapsed. 300 characters with 12 errors over one minute is (300 − 12) ÷ 5 ÷ 1 = 57.6 net WPM.

### What is adjusted WPM?

Adjusted WPM (AWPM) is used in school and exam scoring. It credits all characters typed including errors and reports accuracy separately, so it matches gross WPM rather than net.

### Why do two typing sites give me different scores?

Usually the scoring method and the test length. A 15-second gross score can be 10 WPM higher than a 5-minute net score for the same typist. Compare like with like before deciding you got slower.

### Which WPM should I put on a résumé?

Net WPM with the accuracy and the test length, for example "62 WPM net, 98.5% accuracy, 5-minute test". That is reproducible and it answers the follow-up question before it is asked.
