In data entry, a wrong digit is worse than a slow one, so most employers score net speed - your speed after errors are subtracted - and many set a hard accuracy floor. Practise typing clean the first time rather than relying on backspace, and let speed rise on its own as the keystrokes become automatic.

Take the test at the length the employer uses. Most screening tests run 1 to 5 minutes, so warm up with a 1 minute test and then do a 3 minute or 5 minute run.

Practise with real text that includes numbers, punctuation and capitals, because that is what the passage will contain - not a clean word list. If the role is numeric, train the number pad separately with the 10 key typing test. Sit comfortably, keep your fingers on the home row, and do two or three warm-up runs before your scored attempt so you are at your normal speed.

A data entry record is a set of fields - a name, a reference, a date, an amount - and none of them is a word. Counting characters is the only unit that survives that, which is why postings quote keystrokes per hour or key depressions per hour instead of WPM.

The conversion is arithmetic: 8,000 KPH ≈ 27 WPM, 12,000 KPH ≈ 40 WPM. But the two numbers measure different things in practice, because a character count includes the digits, punctuation and corrections that a prose test quietly ignores.

Type records, not passages. A realistic record is four fields and takes about twelve seconds: name, reference, date, amount. Run ten of them, verify each against the source, and count how many are completely clean - that number, not WPM, is what a data entry manager is buying.

Add the two skills the prose test cannot see: the numeric keypad for amounts (4-5-6 home row, thumb on zero), and Tab to move between fields. Typing a clean record and then pressing the wrong key to advance is a lost record just the same.

## Data entry typing FAQ

### What is a good speed for data entry?

Most data entry jobs ask for 50 to 70 words per minute with 97% or higher accuracy. Numeric-heavy roles are often measured in keystrokes per hour, where 8,000 to 10,000 KPH is the usual requirement.

### How accurate do you need to be for data entry?

Aim for 97% to 99% accuracy. Many employers score net speed (errors subtracted) and set a minimum accuracy threshold, so a clean 60 WPM beats a sloppy 75 WPM.

### How long is a data entry typing test?

Employer screening tests are usually 1 to 5 minutes. Practise at 3 minutes for a balanced check, and use shorter runs to warm up.

### Do data entry jobs use a 10 key test?

Many do. Roles that are mostly numeric use a numeric keypad (10 key) test measured in keystrokes per hour, while text-heavy roles use a words-per-minute passage.

### How can I improve my data entry typing speed?

Practise daily with text that includes numbers, punctuation and capitals, prioritise accuracy over speed, and train the number pad separately if the job is numeric.

### Why is data entry measured in keystrokes rather than WPM?

Because a record is a set of fields - name, reference, date, amount - and none of them is a word. Characters per hour is the only unit that survives that, which is why postings quote 8,000-12,000 KPH.
