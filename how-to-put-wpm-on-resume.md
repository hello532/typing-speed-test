# How to Put WPM on a Résumé

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/how-to-put-wpm-on-resume.html

A typing speed belongs on a résumé only when the job screens for it, and it should be written so it can be verified: method, length and accuracy, not a bare number.

## Where it goes

Put it in the skills section, not the summary. A recruiter scanning for a 50 WPM requirement looks at skills, certifications and the line under the job title - not at a paragraph about your professional philosophy.

Skills line: Typing: 62 WPM net, 98.5% accuracy (5-minute test)
Within a role bullet: "Typed 60+ WPM net while handling 40+ client records daily" - only when the job actually required it.
Certification line: Typing certificate, 55 WPM, proctored at [testing centre], [date] - when a formal certificate is required.

## Why net, with the length

Net WPM subtracts errors, so it is the number a screening test will reproduce. Gross WPM without a length is unverifiable and reads as the flattering version - recruiters who hire typists know the difference.

Adding the test length does two things: it signals that you know a 15-second burst is not a working speed, and it pre-answers the follow-up question. "62 WPM net (5-minute)" is more credible than "70 WPM" even though the number is lower.

## When to leave it off

Leave it off when the role does not screen for typing: management, sales, design, engineering, research. A speed listed against a job that never asked for it looks like filler, and an average number can create a doubt that would not otherwise exist.

The exception is when typing is an explicit part of the posting - a project manager who writes documentation all day can reasonably list 60 WPM if the advert mentions documentation volume.

## What happens in the interview

If you list a number, expect to be tested. Most employers use a short online test, and a handful ask for a certificate. If your listed score came from a one-minute burst on a good day, the live test will read 5-8 WPM lower and the discrepancy is what they will notice.

So list the score you can reproduce on a bad day, at the test length the employer uses. If you do not know the length, assume five minutes and quote that.

## If you have no recent score

Take a five-minute test now, screenshot the result, and use that number - it is current and reproducible. Re-test every six months or after a role change, because typing speed drifts with how much you type.

Keep the screenshot. It costs nothing and it settles the occasional "can you prove that" question without a re-test.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I put my WPM on a résumé?

Yes for clerical, data entry, transcription, dispatch, legal support and customer service roles that screen for typing. No for roles where typing is not part of the job description.

### How do I write WPM on a résumé?

"Typing: 62 WPM net, 98.5% accuracy (5-minute test)". Net, with accuracy and test length, so the number is reproducible rather than flattering.

### Is 60 WPM good enough to list?

Yes. 60 WPM net is about the 66th percentile and above almost every clerical requirement. Below 40 WPM, only list the number if the posting explicitly asks for a lower threshold.

### Should I list gross or net WPM?

Net. Screening tests deduct errors, so a net figure is what they will measure. Gross WPM without a stated method reads as inflated.

### Will an employer verify my typing speed?

Often yes, with a short online test or a certificate. List a score you can reproduce at the same test length, because a live test that reads lower than your résumé is a credibility problem.
