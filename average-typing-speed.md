# Average Typing Speed

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/average-typing-speed.html

The average typing speed is about 40 to 50 words per minute (WPM) with roughly 92% accuracy. 45–60 WPM is good, 60–80 WPM is above average, and only about 1% of people type faster than 100 WPM. Test your own speed below, then compare it with the benchmarks.

The most-cited modern measurement is Dhakal, Feit, Kristensson and Oulasvirta, "Observations on Typing from 136 Million Keystrokes" (CHI 2018). It recorded 136,857,600 keystrokes from 168,960 volunteers and reports a mean of 51.56 WPM with a standard deviation of 20.2, an uncorrected error rate of 1.17%, and 1.173 keystrokes per character. The paper is open at doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174220.

Most pages still quote 40 WPM. That figure comes from older, smaller samples of office workers and students, and it has been copied forward without being re-measured. Both describe real populations: 40 WPM is closer to "typical office worker on a work task", 51.56 WPM to "person who chose to take an online typing test".

Search "average typing speed" and you will find 40, 45, 50, 52 and 60 WPM on the first page. They are not all wrong - they describe different populations, measured different ways. This is the table that reconciles them:

Figure | What it describes | Where it comes from
~40 WPM | Office workers on work tasks, older and smaller samples | The legacy figure quoted in most business and career articles; no single modern study
51.56 WPM | People who chose to take an online typing test | Dhakal et al., CHI 2018 - 136,857,600 keystrokes from 168,960 volunteers, doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174220
36.17 WPM | The same task on a touchscreen | Palin et al., MobileHCI 2019 - 37,370 volunteers, doi.org/10.1145/3338286.3340120
60-70 WPM | "Good typists" in job advertising | Advertised requirements, not a measured population

So the honest answer to "what is the average" is: 51.56 WPM if you mean people who take typing tests, about 40 WPM if you mean office workers on work tasks, and 36 WPM if you mean phones. None of those is a national average, and nobody has measured one recently.

Which should you use? For a job application, use the figure in the posting - employers compare against their own floor, not against a population. For a personal target, use the percentile table above, because it tells you where you sit among people who actually type for a measured minute.

Band | Net WPM | Percentile (approx.) | What it means
Slow | under 26 | bottom 10% | Hunting for keys; typing is the bottleneck
Below average | 26-40 | 10th-28th | Two-finger or untrained touch typing
Average | 40-52 | 28th-50th | Enough for most office work
Good | 52-65 | 50th-75th | Comfortable all-day typing
Fast | 65-78 | 75th-90th | Above most job requirements
Top decile | 78-100 | 90th-99th | Professional typists, developers, transcribers
Exceptional | 100+ | top 1% | Competitive typing, world-record territory

The percentile column is a normal approximation of that distribution, and it reproduces the study's own reported deciles to within about one point. The percentile page does the arithmetic for any score.

Speed peaks in the late teens to thirties in most published samples and declines slowly after that, but the spread within an age band is far larger than the difference between bands. A 55-year-old who touch types beats a 20-year-old who does not, every time.

The practical reading: age is a weak predictor compared with whether someone was ever taught to touch type. If they were not, the gap is trainable at any age - the age breakdown has the numbers by decade.

Data entry: 45-65 WPM, often quoted as 13,500-19,500 KPH, with 97-98% accuracy.
Administrative and secretarial: 50-70 WPM depending on seniority.
Transcription: 65-80 WPM sustained over audio, which is harder than the same speed over visible text.
Software developers: no formal requirement; most sit above 70 WPM, and code typing differs because of brackets and symbols.
Writers and journalists: 60-80 WPM, where the limit is usually thinking rather than typing.

Role-by-role figures, in the units employers actually use, are on the typing speed requirements page.

Test length: a 15-second burst reads 5-10 WPM higher than a five-minute run. Text type: random words score lower than sentences, code lower again, digits are a different skill. Scoring: gross WPM is higher than net. Keyboard: a laptop chiclet keyboard is slower than a mechanical one for most people.

So before concluding you are below average, check what you measured. A five-minute net score on sentences is what this page's bands refer to; a one-minute gross score on random words is not comparable.

Run a five-minute sentence test, then read net WPM and accuracy from the results panel. Compare the net figure against the table above, and check whether accuracy is inside the 97-98% band most employers require - a fast score at 92% accuracy is not the same asset as a slightly slower clean one.

## Typing test FAQ

### What is the average typing speed?

51.56 WPM in the largest published measurement, from 136,857,600 keystrokes recorded from 168,960 volunteers (Dhakal et al., CHI 2018). The older 40 WPM figure reflects office-worker samples rather than self-selected test takers.

### Is 40 WPM good?

It is around the 28th percentile of the CHI 2018 distribution and clears the floor for most clerical and reception roles. It is below the 50-70 WPM band that legal, medical and transcription roles ask for.

### What is a fast typing speed?

Above 78 WPM puts you in the top 10% of the measured population. Above 100 WPM is roughly the top 1%, and competitive typists exceed 130 WPM in short bursts on known text.

### Does typing speed decline with age?

Slightly, and far less than the variation between people of the same age. Whether someone was taught touch typing predicts speed much better than age does.

### Why do I score lower on longer tests?

The first thirty seconds are warm-up pace you cannot hold. A five-minute net score is typically 5-10 WPM below a 15-second burst, and the longer number is the one that reflects a working day.

### How is typing speed measured?

Correct characters divided by five, then divided by the minutes elapsed. Five characters count as one word. Net WPM subtracts errors first; gross WPM does not.

### What is the average WPM for an adult?

51.56 WPM in the largest published measurement (168,960 volunteers, CHI 2018). The commonly quoted 40 WPM comes from older office-worker samples rather than self-selected test takers.

### What is the average typing speed for a beginner?

20-30 WPM with 90-95% accuracy is typical for an untrained adult. With ten minutes of daily practice, 35-45 WPM within three months is a realistic progression.

### Why do different sites quote different average typing speeds?

They measure different populations: about 40 WPM for office workers on work tasks (the legacy figure), 51.56 WPM for people who take online typing tests (CHI 2018, n=168,960), and 36.17 WPM for phones (MobileHCI 2019).
