# Average Typing Speed

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/average-typing-speed.html

The average typing speed is about 40 to 50 words per minute (WPM) with roughly 92% accuracy. 45–60 WPM is good, 60–80 WPM is above average, and only about 1% of people type faster than 100 WPM. Test your own speed below, then compare it with the benchmarks.

## What is the average typing speed?

The average typing speed is about 52 words per minute (WPM) in the largest published sample of everyday typists, with a wide spread: the standard deviation is 20 WPM, so a large share of adults sit between 30 and 70. The familiar “40–50 WPM” band comes from older office-work benchmarks and still describes what many jobs ask for; it is a floor, not the middle. A speed of 45–60 WPM is considered good for office work, 60–80 WPM is above average, and roughly the top 10% clear 78 WPM. The table below shows the bands, and the sources section shows the study behind the headline number.

Skill level | Speed (WPM) | What it means
Beginner | Under 30 WPM | Still learning; hunt-and-peck on many keys
Below average | 30-45 WPM | Functional but slow; typing takes conscious effort
Average | 40-50 WPM | Where most adults land without practice
Good / above average | 60-80 WPM | Comfortable; meets most office job requirements
Fast | 80-100 WPM | Top ~10% of typists
Advanced / elite | 100+ WPM | Top ~1%; only about 1% type over 100 WPM

## Average typing speed by age

Typing speed is shaped more by how long someone has typed daily than by age itself, but it does follow a rough curve: it climbs through the teens, peaks in the late teens to twenties, then eases slightly later in life. These are approximate averages.

Age group | Average WPM | Notes
Under 13 | 15-30 WPM | Still building muscle memory; wide variance
13-18 | 35-55 WPM | Heavy phone users are often slower than expected
18-30 | 50-65 WPM | Peak learning years; fastest improvement
30-45 | 45-60 WPM | Stable; reflects accumulated work habits
45-60 | 40-55 WPM | Slight drop in raw speed, often higher accuracy
60+ | 30-45 WPM | Motor slowdown; practice still helps

## Typing speed by profession

Different jobs demand very different typing speeds. Roles where typing is the job - data entry, transcription, legal and executive support - expect well above average, while general office work is fine around 40–55 WPM. Accuracy usually matters as much as raw speed.

General office work | 40-55 WPM | 95%+
Customer support / chat | 50-65 WPM | 97%+
Administrative assistant | 50-70 WPM | 97%+

Actual requirements vary by employer, test software and scoring rule, so treat the bands as a starting point for practice rather than a guarantee of any specific job threshold.

## How to type faster than average

If you are below the speed you want, the fastest way up is touch typing: use all ten fingers from the home row (ASDF JKL;) without looking at the keyboard. Prioritise accuracy over speed, practise 10–15 minutes a day, and keep your wrists relaxed. Most people add 10–20 WPM within a few weeks of daily practice.

The headline figure on this page — a mean of about 52 WPM across a large sample of everyday typists — matches the largest published study of modern typing: Dhakal, Feit, Kristensson and Oulasvirta, Observations on Typing from 136 Million Keystrokes (CHI 2018), which recorded 136,857,600 keystrokes from 168,960 volunteers and reports a mean of 51.56 WPM (SD 20.2) and a mean uncorrected error rate of 1.17%. The full paper is open at doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174220.

That study also explains why the common “40–50 WPM” band understates the middle of the distribution: its sample is self-selected people who care about typing, and its mean sits above the office-work band that older textbook figures quote. Where this page gives a narrower band for a job or an age group, it is an estimate from the ranges those roles advertise, not a measured sample — and it is labelled as such.

Two other sources shape the advice here. The claim that finger consistency and keypress preparation predict speed, rather than which finger presses which key, comes from Feit, Weir and Oulasvirta’s motion-capture work on 36 typists (CHI 2018, discussed alongside the dataset above). The historical claim that alternative layouts such as Dvorak produce only modest gains after retraining comes from the published layout comparisons summarised on our keyboard layouts page.

## Typing test FAQ

### What is the average typing speed?

The average typing speed is about 40–50 words per minute (WPM) with roughly 92% accuracy. Most adults who have not practised deliberately settle between 40 and 60 WPM.

### What is a good typing speed?

A good typing speed is 45–60 WPM. 60–80 WPM is above average and meets most office job requirements, while over 70 WPM is advanced. Only about 1% of people type faster than 100 WPM.

### Is 40 WPM good?

40 WPM is right around the adult average and is fine for everyday computer use. If you are accurate at 40 WPM, 60 WPM is a realistic next target with regular touch-typing practice.

### What is the fastest typing speed?

On a normal QWERTY keyboard the fastest typists reach 150+ WPM. The highest reliably recorded speeds belong to Barbara Blackburn, who sustained 212 WPM on a Dvorak keyboard (a Guinness record), and Stella Pajunas, timed at 216 WPM back in 1946. Court reporters exceed 225 WPM, but on a specialised stenotype machine using shorthand chords, not a standard keyboard. For context the average is about 40 WPM, and professional typists aim for 97%+ accuracy.

### How is typing speed measured?

Typing speed is measured in words per minute (WPM), where one word equals five characters including spaces. Net WPM subtracts errors, so WPM = (correct characters ÷ 5) ÷ minutes.

### How can I type faster than average?

Touch type with all ten fingers from the home row (ASDF JKL;), prioritise accuracy over speed, and practise 10–15 minutes a day. Speed follows once your accuracy is consistently above 95%.
