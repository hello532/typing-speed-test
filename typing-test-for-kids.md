## What is a good typing speed for kids by age?

Children type more slowly than adults, which is completely normal - the goal at this age is correct finger habits, not speed. These are rough averages; a child who touch types a little slower will overtake a faster hunt-and-peck typist within months.

Age | Average WPM | Notes
Under 8 | 8-15 WPM | First letters; still finding keys
8-10 | 12-25 WPM | Home row settling in
11-13 | 15-30 WPM | Muscle memory building fast
13-15 | 25-45 WPM | Approaching adult beginner range

## Typing test FAQ

### What is a good typing speed for a child?

Children under 13 typically type 15–30 WPM and teenagers 35–55 WPM. Speed matters far less than building correct touch-typing habits early, so focus on accuracy and finger placement first.

### Is this typing test free and safe for kids?

Yes. It is completely free, needs no sign-up or account, shows no ads inside the typing area, and stores results only in the browser &mdash; nothing is sent to a server.

### At what age should kids learn to type?

Most children can start learning touch typing around ages 6–7, once their hands are big enough to reach the home row. Short, playful sessions work far better than long ones at this age.

### How long should kids practice typing?

Short daily sessions of 10–15 minutes build muscle memory faster than occasional long ones. Keep it fun and stop before it feels like a chore.

### How can my child type without looking?

Have them rest their fingers on the home row (ASDF for the left hand, JKL; for the right), use the on-screen keyboard and hand guide below, and resist peeking. Not looking at the keys is the whole skill of touch typing.
