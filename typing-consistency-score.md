# Typing Consistency Score

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/typing-consistency-score.html

Two typists can average 60 WPM and be very different: one runs 58-62 the whole minute, the other swings between 40 and 80. Consistency is the figure that separates them, and it predicts your five-minute score better than your peak does.

Speed is sampled once per second during the round. Let those samples be a series of speeds with mean m. Consistency is one minus the coefficient of variation, expressed as a percentage:

consistency = 100 × (1 − standard deviation ÷ mean)

So a round that holds 60 WPM with a standard deviation of 3 reads 95. A round that averages 60 but swings with a standard deviation of 12 reads 80. It is clamped to 0-100, and a round with fewer than two samples reports 100 rather than an error.

The number appears in the results panel as Consistency (labeled 安定度 / 稳定性 / استحکام in other interface languages) and in the share image.

Average WPM is an average of a curve, and the curve has shape. A typist whose speed collapses in the last ten seconds has a stamina problem; one whose speed spikes at the start and settles has a pacing problem; one whose speed oscillates throughout is reading word by word instead of ahead.

Consistency separates those cases. It also explains a common frustration: two people with the same one-minute score can have completely different five-minute scores, and the one with the higher consistency usually keeps more of it.

Consistency | What it looks like | What to do
90-100 | Nearly flat; the average is the truth | Nothing - work on speed instead
80-89 | Mild variation, usually around difficult words | Drill the words that cause the dips
70-79 | Visible dips; the average overstates your sustainable pace | Slow down 10% and hold it for a full minute
under 70 | Large swings, often burst-then-recover | Accuracy work first; the swings are usually corrections

Metronome typing. Type to a steady rhythm - one word per beat - for 30 seconds. The goal is not speed but flatness; you will feel the urges to sprint and to recover.
Slow-hold rounds. Pick a pace you can hold for the entire round without a single sprint, and finish at exactly that pace. Most typists discover their sustainable pace is 10-15% below their peak.
Dip hunting. After a round, look at the graph and find the lowest second. Type the words around that point again, slowly. Dips concentrate on the same few sequences - usually one hand doing two awkward reaches.

On very short rounds (15 seconds) there are too few samples for the figure to mean much. On very hard text, low consistency is expected and correct - the dips are the difficulty, not a technique failure.

For benchmarking, keep using net WPM plus accuracy. Consistency is a diagnostic for how you got the number, not a replacement for it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good typing consistency score?

Above 85 is steady, 90+ is nearly flat. Below 70 means your speed swings enough that the average overstates what you can sustain over five minutes.

### How is consistency calculated?

One minus the coefficient of variation of your per-second WPM, as a percentage: 100 × (1 − standard deviation ÷ mean). It is clamped to 0-100.

### Does consistency matter more than speed?

For sustained work, yes. A steady 55 holds up over a ten-minute test; a spiky 60 often drops to 48. For passing a short screening test, speed matters more.

### Why is my consistency low even when my accuracy is high?

Accuracy measures errors, consistency measures rhythm. You can be accurate and still accelerate and stall, usually because you read word by word rather than looking ahead.

### Which drill helps most?

Slow-hold rounds: pick a pace you can maintain for the whole round and finish at exactly that pace. It teaches the difference between peak and sustainable speed.
