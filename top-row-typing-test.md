# Top Row Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/top-row-typing-test.html

Drill the top row &mdash; Q W E R T Y U I O P. Ten keys, six of the twelve most common English letters. Type the passage with the finger guide on, then read your WPM and accuracy.

The top row is the first row of letters under the number keys: Q W E R T Y U I O P. In standard touch typing the left hand owns Q W E R T (pinky, ring, middle, index, index) and the right hand owns Y U I O P (index, index, middle, ring, pinky).

Unlike the home row, which you can find by the raised bumps on F and J, the top row has no tactile landmark. You get there by reaching up from home and coming straight back, never by looking down.

Six of the twelve most common letters in English live on this row: E, T, R, U, I, O. That is more frequent letters than the home row and the bottom row each carry, so top-row hesitation costs you time in nearly every word you type.

It also holds two of the three hardest reaches on a QWERTY board, Q and P, both for the little fingers. Those two keys are where most accuracy is silently lost, because a missed Q or P rarely looks like a mistake until you read the word back.

Reaching with the wrong finger. T is left index and Y is right index, so the hand has to split cleanly in the middle. Typing T with the right hand or Y with the left breaks the whole row.

Losing home position. If the hand does not return to A S D F and J K L ; after each reach, every following letter starts from the wrong place. Slow the row down until the return is automatic, then raise speed.

## Top row FAQ

### What are the top row keys?

Q, W, E, R, T, Y, U, I, O, P. The left hand types Q W E R T and the right hand types Y U I O P.

### Which fingers type the top row?

Left: pinky on Q, ring on W, middle on E, index on R and T. Right: index on Y and U, middle on I, ring on O, pinky on P.

### Why is the top row hard to learn?

It has no raised bumps, so there is no tactile way to find it. You locate it by reaching up from the home row and returning, which only becomes reliable with repetition.

### Should I practise the top row before or after the home row?

After. Learn the home row first so your fingers have a base to return to, then add this row, then the bottom row.

### How long should I drill one row?

Short rounds of five to ten minutes beat long sessions, because the goal is a clean return to home rather than volume. Stop when accuracy drops below about 95 percent.
