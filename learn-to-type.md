## Your first lesson in learning to type

Rest your left fingers on A S D F and your right fingers on J K L ;, thumbs on the space bar. Feel the small bumps on F and J; they are the only map a beginner needs. Type words made from the home row only, slowly, and let each finger return home after every key. If you must look, look after the word, never in the middle of it. That is the entire first lesson, and everything after it is just practice.

## How long does it take to learn to type?

Most people own the home row in a week and reach a comfortable 40 to 60 words per minute in three to four weeks of daily practice. The mistake beginners make is chasing speed early; accuracy is the foundation and speed is the building that arrives later. End every session on your slowest, most careful line, and your hands will learn faster than you expect.

## Learn to type FAQ

### Can I learn to type for free?

Yes. This course is completely free, with no sign up, no account, and no paywall. Every lesson is a timed practice you can repeat as often as you like, and your results stay in your own browser.

### I have never touched a keyboard. Can I still learn to type?

That is exactly who this page is for. It starts at the home row, assumes nothing, and adds one row at a time. Being slow in the first week is not a setback; it is the lesson.

### What is the first thing I should learn?

The home row: A S D F J K L ; with your thumbs on the space bar. Find the bumps on F and J with your index fingers, and practice words made only from those keys until you never look down.

### How long until I can type without looking?

Usually one to two weeks for the home row, and three to four weeks before the whole keyboard feels automatic. Covering the keyboard with a cloth speeds this up, because it removes the option of cheating.

### Should I focus on speed or accuracy while learning?

Accuracy, always. Speed is a byproduct of accurate repetition. A fast wrong keystroke teaches your hand the wrong address for a key, which you then have to unlearn.

### How many minutes a day should I practice?

Ten to fifteen minutes every day. Short daily practice builds muscle memory far better than one long weekly session, because the hands learn by frequency, not by total minutes.
