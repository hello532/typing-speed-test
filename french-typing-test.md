# French Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/french-typing-test.html

Real French text with the characters that decide accuracy: é, è, ê, à, ç, ù, œ and the space before a colon. Works on an AZERTY keyboard, and the score is reported in mots par minute and caractères par minute.

Three things. First, accents: é, è, ê, à, ç and ù each have their own key on AZERTY, and œ (as in cœur) is a separate character again. Second, punctuation spacing: French typography puts a space before : ; ! ? and inside guillemets, so a correct sentence has more keystrokes than the English equivalent.

Third, apostrophes. French elides constantly (l'oiseau, qu'il, d'accord), and the apostrophe key on AZERTY sits on the fourth row, reached with the right little finger. That single reach accounts for a large share of errors in French typing tests.

French tests report mots par minute (MPM) and caractères par minute (CPM). The word count uses five characters per word, the same convention as English, so a French and an English score are comparable - provided both tests count characters the same way.

French keyboards are AZERTY: A and Q swap, Z and W swap, M moves next to L, and the accented letters replace the number row. If you use an AZERTY keyboard, this test works directly, because the browser receives the character your system produces.

The detail that catches people is the space before double punctuation. A French sentence writes « Bonjour : voici » with a space before the colon, not « Bonjour: voici ». Typing tests that use real French text include that space, and typists trained on English punctuation lose accuracy on it until they notice.

One-minute rounds first, then five minutes once accuracy is above 97%. Add a round with quotes once a week, because that is where the guillemets and the double punctuation appear together.

French office and secrétariat postings commonly quote 40-50 mots par minute, and some public-sector concours include a bureautique exercise rather than a formal typing score. Saisie de données roles are quoted in caractères par minute or keystrokes per hour - convert with the WPM to KPH converter.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good French typing speed?

40-50 mots par minute (200-250 caractères par minute) is solid for office work. Secretarial and data-entry roles often ask for 50-60 MPM, with accuracy above 97%.

### Does this test work on an AZERTY keyboard?

Yes. The test compares the characters your operating system produces, so accented letters and ç work directly on an AZERTY layout without configuration.

### Why does French punctuation slow me down?

French typography requires a space before : ; ! ? and uses guillemets, which adds keystrokes. If you learned English punctuation, the extra space is a habit you have to build.

### How do I type œ and the accents?

On AZERTY, accents have their own keys and œ is typed with a dedicated combination. Add the French layout in your OS settings rather than using Alt codes, which do not train the right motion.

### How many words per minute is 250 caractères?

Fifty mots par minute. Characters ÷ 5 = words, the same convention used by English typing tests, so the two scores are comparable.
