# Why Am I Stuck at the Same Typing Speed?

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/why-am-i-stuck-at-the-same-typing-speed.html

A plateau is not one problem. Where you are stuck tells you what is stuck: at 40 it is usually the look-at-keyboard habit, at 60 it is corrections, at 80 it is consistency. Here is the diagnosis by band.

On this page

First, check that you are actually stuck
Stuck at 30-40: the habit ceiling
Stuck at 50: the reading bottleneck
Stuck at 60: the accuracy tax
Stuck at 70-80: variance, not peak
If none of that applies

Measure the same way three times: same length, same text mode, same time of day. If the three scores sit within 3 WPM, you are consistent and the plateau is real. If they swing by 10, you do not have a plateau - you have noisy measurement, and the fix is a fixed protocol rather than a new drill.

Then check the length. A one-minute score improves faster than a five-minute score, so a plateau on a five-minute test looks like progress on a one-minute one. Decide which number you are tracking and stay with it.

Almost always two habits: looking at the keyboard, and backspacing through every slip. Both are cheap to fix and both feel worse before they feel better.

Covered rounds: hands hidden, 30 seconds, once a day. Expect accuracy to drop 10-20% for a week.
No-backspace rounds: finish with mistakes in them. Most people at this level gain 3-5 WPM immediately.

At 50 WPM your fingers are no longer the limit; your eyes are. You read the word you are about to type, so the hands wait for the eyes on every word. Faster typists read one to three words ahead and let the hands run on a buffer.

Drill: before starting a line, read it silently once, then type it without re-reading. It feels wrong for three rounds and then it stops feeling wrong. Add 15-second bursts to raise your top end, because a 50 average with a 55 peak has no room to grow.

At 60 WPM a 2% error rate costs about 5-6 net WPM over a five-minute test. That is the arithmetic that keeps people at 55-60 for months: they gain speed, lose accuracy, and net out where they started.

Drill: 30-second rounds at 90% of your maximum, with an error budget of one. Stop the round when the budget is blown rather than pushing to the timer. The skill being trained is accuracy at speed, which is different from accuracy at rest.

Above 70 the ceiling is consistency. Ten rounds might range from 68 to 84; the average is 74 and the peak is irrelevant. Narrowing the spread is what raises the average.

Drill: type ten one-minute rounds and record all ten. Work on the lowest quintile - the rounds where you were tired, distracted or on a hard passage. Slow-hold rounds (pick a pace, hold it exactly) and hard-text rounds do more here than any speed drill.

Then the constraint is probably outside the keyboard: sleep, screen position, keyboard feel, or the fact that you only practise in one-minute bursts and never test endurance. Try one ten-minute round a week - many "plateaus" turn out to be a stamina problem that a short test cannot see.

The band-by-band plans are on the 40, 50, 60 and 70 WPM pages; the diagnostic line is consistency.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why has my typing speed not improved in months?

Usually because the practice repeats what you already do well. Plateaus have band-specific causes: habits at 40, reading ahead at 50, error tax at 60, variance at 70+.

### How often should I practise to break a plateau?

Ten to fifteen minutes a day, with one variable changed per week. Longer sessions at the same routine mostly build fatigue.

### Does a new keyboard help?

Slightly, and briefly. A mechanical keyboard can be worth 3-8 WPM for some typists, but it will not break a plateau caused by looking down or reading word by word.

### Should I switch to Dvorak or Colemak?

Not to break a plateau. The switch costs weeks of being slower, and most people return to the same speed on the new layout because the limiting habit moved with them.

### How long does a plateau usually last?

Two to six weeks if you change the drill, months if you keep repeating the same practice. The signal that a drill is working is accuracy rising before speed does.
