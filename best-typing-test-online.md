## monkeytype: the minimalist's favourite

monkeytype is the design darling of the typing world: a clean, fast, endlessly customizable test with themes, modes and a huge community. It needs no account for the core test, supports many test languages and modes, and its smooth interface is genuinely the best-feeling in the category.

Its trade-offs: weak-key analysis is basic compared to dedicated trainers, there are no structured word lists or lessons, and the sheer number of options can overwhelm a beginner who just wants to know if they're getting faster. Pick monkeytype if you already know how to type and want the most polished daily driver.

## 10fastfingers: the global multiplayer giant

10fastfingers quietly became the biggest typing site outside the English-speaking world by doing two things brilliantly: a multiplayer race mode that turns practice into competition, and a staggering language matrix — 45+ interface languages crossed with 50+ test languages. In Indonesia, Vietnam and Turkey it is the default typing test.

Its trade-offs: the interface feels dated, per-key weakness analysis is limited, and content beyond the test (guides, word lists) is thin. Pick 10fastfingers if you want competitive racing or practice in a language other than the big five.

## typing.com: the classroom standard

typing.com is built for schools: complete curricula, teacher dashboards, student management, and standards-aligned lessons from kindergarten to adult. For structured learning with accountability, nothing else in the free tier comes close.

Its trade-offs: most of the good stuff sits behind a free-but-required account, the site is heavy (ad-supported), and casual users who just want a quick speed check will find it overkill. Pick typing.com for a child learning properly, a classroom, or if you want a guided course rather than a test.

## keybr and TypingTest.com: the two extremes

keybr is the algorithmic coach: it generates practice from your own error patterns, letter by letter, with a clean no-account interface. It's excellent for building touch-typing fundamentals, though it offers no languages beyond practice variants and no real-text passages early on.

TypingTest.com is the opposite extreme: the internet's original quick check — one page, one test, zero clutter, zero account. It's English-only with no analysis, but as a ten-second 'how fast am I today' it has worked since 1999. For anything beyond the quick check, look at the others above.

## Typing.Rerivo: multilingual practice with measurement built in

Full disclosure: this is our site, so read this row with appropriate skepticism — but here's what it does differently. The test runs in 12 languages with real practice text per language (not translated English), every round ends with a per-key weak-key report, and it ships with 200-word frequency lists per language, a printable certificate, and 30+ guides on speed, exams and technique. No sign up, no install, works offline in your browser.

Its trade-offs: it's the newest site here, so there's no community or multiplayer yet, and the design is functional rather than flashy. Pick it if you type in more than one language, you're preparing for an employment typing test, or you want your practice to come with a measurement plan attached. ## How to choose in ten seconds

Want the prettiest daily test? monkeytype. Want to race people? 10fastfingers. Teaching a class or a child? typing.com. Learning finger placement from zero? keybr. A ten-second speed check? TypingTest.com. Multilingual practice, exam prep, or measured improvement with weak-key data? Typing.Rerivo.

The honest meta-answer: all of them are free, so use two. A daily measurement on one site and practice on another keeps both scores honest — and if you ever need to prove your speed, a typing certificate from a timed, dated test beats any screenshot.

## Best typing test FAQ

### What is the best free typing test online?

It depends on your goal: monkeytype for design and daily use, 10fastfingers for multiplayer and non-English languages, typing.com for classrooms and courses, keybr for learning finger placement, TypingTest.com for a zero-clutter quick check, and Typing.Rerivo for multilingual practice with per-key weakness analysis.

### Which typing test needs no sign up?

Typing.Rerivo, monkeytype, 10fastfingers, keybr and TypingTest.com all work with no account. typing.com requires a free account for its lessons and courses, though its basic test is open.

### Which typing test has the most languages?

10fastfingers has the broadest matrix — 45+ interface languages crossed with 50+ test languages. Typing.Rerivo offers 12 test languages with real practice text per language plus top-200 word lists for each.

### Which typing test is best for improving, not just measuring?

Look for per-key weakness analysis and structured practice: keybr adapts to your errors letter by letter, typing.com has full courses, and Typing.Rerivo gives a weak-key report after every round plus word lists and drills targeting exactly those keys.

### Are these typing tests really free?

Yes, all six have usable free tiers. typing.com and monkeytype offer paid tiers (ad-free/premium features); Typing.Rerivo, keybr and TypingTest.com are free with no account and no paywall.
