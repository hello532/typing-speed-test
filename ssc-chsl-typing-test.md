# SSC CHSL Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/ssc-chsl-typing-test.html

Two different skill tests sit inside SSC CHSL: 35 WPM English or 30 WPM Hindi for LDC and JSA posts, and 8000 key depressions per hour for DEO posts. They are scored differently and rehearsed differently.

## The two tests, side by side

Post | Standard | Duration | Scoring
LDC / JSA | 35 WPM English or 30 WPM Hindi | 10 minutes | Words per minute with an error allowance
DEO / DEO Grade A | 8000 key depressions per hour | 15 minutes | Total key depressions against an 8000/hour rate

8000 key depressions per hour is about 26.7 WPM under the five-character convention, but it is measured as raw keystrokes including corrections you make, so a typist who backspaces constantly loses ground even at a good words-per-minute rate.

## Error allowance is where candidates fail

The typing test is qualifying: you either clear the standard or you do not, and the score does not add to your merit total. That changes the strategy completely. A candidate chasing 45 WPM with 12% errors fails; a candidate at 37 WPM with 3% errors passes.

Practise with the error budget in view: type at a pace where you can hold mistakes under the allowance, and never let a correction spree eat the clock. On the 10-minute LDC test, thirty seconds lost to backspacing is roughly 18 words you never typed.

## Keyboard and layout rules

The test is taken on a standard QWERTY keyboard provided at the centre. Hindi candidates may opt for Krutidev or Remington layout depending on the notice in force, and the layout is usually chosen at the application stage — check your admit card and the current notice rather than assuming.

Because the machine is not yours, rehearse on something other than your laptop at least twice before the exam. Key travel and spacing change your error rate more than practice volume does.

## A four-week plan

Week 1: ten-minute rounds at a comfortable pace, recording accuracy only. Week 2: same length, push until errors cross the allowance, then back off 3 WPM — that is your exam pace. Week 3: alternate sentences and passages with punctuation, because the actual passage includes capitals and full stops. Week 4: full-length simulations at the same time of day as your shift, with no pause button.

The DEO candidate should add a daily 15-minute numbers round, since key depressions include digits and the numeric reach is the slowest part of the keyboard for most people.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the typing speed required for SSC CHSL?

35 words per minute in English or 30 words per minute in Hindi for LDC and JSA posts, and 8000 key depressions per hour for DEO posts. Both are qualifying tests.

### Is the SSC CHSL typing test qualifying or merit?

Qualifying. It does not add marks to your total; you either meet the standard or you do not. That is why accuracy matters more than a high raw speed.

### How long is the SSC CHSL typing test?

Ten minutes for LDC and JSA, fifteen minutes for DEO. Practise at the full length rather than in one-minute bursts, because the error rate at minute nine is what decides the result.

### How many mistakes are allowed?

A small error allowance applies, commonly around 5% of the words typed, and it is specified in the notice for that cycle. Check the current notice: the allowance and the passage length change between years.

### Can I use my own keyboard?

No. The test is conducted on centre equipment. Rehearse on a different keyboard at least twice so key travel and spacing are not a surprise on the day.
