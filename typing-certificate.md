This page generates a typing certificate from your most recent round. It is free, needs no account, and is produced entirely in your browser - nothing is uploaded, which is also why there is no verification code on it.

The certificate is a printable record of one test: who typed, when, at what speed and accuracy, on what test length. It is a convenience for applications that want a number, not an accredited credential. The honest version of that distinction is spelled out on the certificate acceptance page.

Field | Content | Why it is there
Name | Whatever you type in the name field | An employer needs to attach the number to a person
Date | The date you generated it | Typing speed drifts; a dated result is checkable
WPM | Net words per minute from that round | The number most postings quote
CPM | Characters per minute | Data-entry roles quote characters, not words
Accuracy | Percentage of correct characters | The part employers actually weight
Length and mode | e.g. 5 minutes, sentences | Makes the score comparable - a 15-second burst is not

Paper: A4 or US Letter both work; the layout uses standard margins and no bleed.
Colour: it prints correctly in black and white, which is what most offices will do anyway.
Browser print settings: turn off headers and footers so the URL and date stamps do not appear over the artwork.
Digital copy: print to PDF rather than photographing the screen - the text stays selectable and the file stays small.

If the result panel is still open, the certificate uses that round. If you have closed it, take a new round first: the certificate is generated from the round in the current session, not from your history.

Use a five-minute test and quote the net figure. A one-minute result reads 5-10 WPM higher and is the first thing a careful employer discounts. If you are applying for a role quoted in keystrokes per hour, convert first: 45 WPM is 13,500 KPH, and the converter does it both ways.

If your accuracy is below 97%, run an accuracy round before generating the certificate. A certificate showing 55 WPM at 92% invites the question of how many of those words were right.

Private-sector recruiters commonly accept a dated result as a screening signal and then test you themselves. Public-sector, court, dispatch and hospital roles usually require a proctored test or a certificate from an approved centre, and the posting states the wording.

Plan for the re-test: if your certificate says 58 and their test reads 52, that is normal and consistent. If it says 70 because you quoted a 15-second burst, it looks like a discrepancy.

## Typing certificate FAQ

### Is the typing certificate free?

Yes. It is generated in your browser from your last round, with no account, no payment and no watermark.

### How do I get my typing certificate?

Finish a round, open the result panel and choose the certificate option. It composes from that round - name, date, WPM, CPM, accuracy, length and mode - and opens the browser print dialogue.

### What WPM do I need for a typing certificate?

There is no minimum: the certificate reports whatever the round measured. For job applications, a five-minute net score of 40+ WPM is the usual useful threshold, with accuracy at 97% or better.

### Can I use it as proof for a job or school?

Some private employers and schools accept a dated, named result. Government, court, dispatch and hospital roles usually require a proctored test or a certificate from an approved centre.

### Does it work for tests longer than one minute?

Yes. The certificate records the length and mode of the round, so a five-minute or ten-minute result is reported as such.

### Where is my typing data stored?

In your browser only. There is no server-side storage, no account and no lookup, which is also why the certificate carries no verification ID.
