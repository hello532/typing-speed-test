## How pre-employment typing tests work

Employer typing tests are timed, usually 1 to 5 minutes, with 3 minutes a common length. They score net WPM - your speed after errors are subtracted - so accuracy counts as much as speed. Some tests disable the backspace key, which makes careful typing even more important.

Practise at the right length with the 1 minute, 3 minute or 5 minute tests, and check your accuracy.

## How to pass a typing test for a job

Prioritise accuracy over raw speed - most employers prefer 60 WPM at 99% accuracy over 75 WPM at 92%. Practise with real sentences, punctuation and numbers rather than simple word lists, take several tests to find a reliable average, and warm up for a minute first. ## Typing test FAQ

### What is a good typing speed for a job?

Most office jobs expect 50–60 WPM. Data entry, transcription and legal roles ask for 60–80 WPM, usually with 95–98% accuracy. Specialised roles can require 90+ WPM.

### What WPM do data entry jobs require?

Data entry roles commonly require 60–80 WPM with 98%+ accuracy. Numeric data entry is often measured in characters per minute instead, with at least 165 CPM as a baseline.

### How long is a pre-employment typing test?

Employer typing tests are usually 1 to 5 minutes long, with 3 minutes a common length. Longer tests give a more reliable score because they average out short bursts of speed and fatigue.

### Is net WPM or gross WPM used for jobs?

Employers use net WPM, which subtracts your errors, not gross WPM. A posting that says 60 WPM required almost always means 60 net WPM with high accuracy.

### How can I prepare for an employment typing test?

Practise a timed test at the length the employer uses, prioritise accuracy over raw speed, and get used to typing real sentences with punctuation and numbers rather than simple word lists.

### Is this the same as a typing assessment or typing exam?

Functionally yes. A pre-employment typing assessment, a typing exam and a job typing test all measure the same two numbers — net WPM and accuracy — under timed conditions. Practising here with one-attempt rounds is the closest rehearsal you can get without the recruiter watching.
