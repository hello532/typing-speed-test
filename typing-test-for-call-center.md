# Typing Test for Call Center Jobs

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/typing-test-for-call-center.html

Call centre work is typing while listening. The requirement is lower than office typing — usually 30–45 WPM — but accuracy on names and reference numbers is what separates the shortlist from the reject pile.

## Targets by role

Role | Typing target | Why
Inbound voice support | 30–40 WPM | Short call notes, CRM fields, reference numbers
Outbound sales | 30–40 WPM | Disposition codes and call-back notes
Chat and email support | 40–55 WPM | The conversation itself is typed, often two at once
Technical support | 40–50 WPM | Ticket text, error codes, device identifiers
Back-office and escalations | 45–55 WPM | Case notes and customer correspondence

Voice roles are deliberately lenient on speed: nobody types a paragraph while a customer is mid-sentence. What the test is really checking is that you can capture a name, a postcode and a reference without asking the caller to repeat themselves.

## The skill nobody practises: typing while listening

Reading and copying is a different task from hearing and recording. In the second, your eyes are free but your buffer is short — people drop the last two digits of a phone number because they were still typing the previous word.

Rehearse it directly: play a podcast or read a sentence aloud, then type it from memory in one pass and check it. Do five of these a day for a week. Accuracy on heard digits improves faster than raw speed, and it is the part the job actually punishes.

## Numbers are where accuracy breaks

A transcription error on a card number, an order reference or a callback number turns into a second call, and second calls are the metric every contact centre tracks. Practise the Numbers mode for two minutes a day and check your error count rather than your speed.

If your accuracy on digits is below 97%, stop chasing speed for a week. The arithmetic is unforgiving: at 40 WPM with 94% accuracy you are generating about two and a half errors a minute, most of them in exactly the fields that matter.

## How to take the screening test

Ask whether the test is scored gross or net, and whether the timer is visible. If it is, do not watch it — the countdown costs more accuracy than it buys speed.

Type in short bursts and release the keys between words. Call-centre typing rewards a steady rhythm over a fast start, because the test is usually one to three minutes and the notes you will type in the job are never longer than a sentence or two.

## Frequently asked questions

### What WPM do call centre jobs require?

Usually 30–45 WPM for voice roles and 40–55 WPM for chat and email support, with accuracy above 95%. The numbers are lower than general office typing because most notes are short.

### Is 30 WPM enough for a call centre?

For inbound voice support, often yes, provided your accuracy on names and numbers is high. Chat support and technical roles ask for more because the conversation itself is typed.

### How do I practise typing while listening?

Play audio or read a sentence aloud once, then type it from memory in a single pass and check it. Five repetitions a day for a week improves captured-digit accuracy noticeably.

### Do call centre tests include numbers and punctuation?

Frequently. Reference numbers, postcodes and callback numbers are the realistic content. Practise the Numbers mode as well as sentences so the digit row is not a surprise.

### What accuracy is expected?

95% is the usual floor and 98% is comfortable. Errors in customer identifiers create repeat calls, so screening tools often weight accuracy more heavily than speed for these roles.
