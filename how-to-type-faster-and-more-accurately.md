# How to Type Faster and More Accurately

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/how-to-type-faster-and-more-accurately.html

How to type faster and more accurately at the same time is not a trade-off — once you see the error-cost math, the two goals become the same goal.

## The error-cost math most typists never do

Every typing error costs three times: the mistyped character does not count, the backspace costs a keystroke, and the retype costs another. At 60 WPM (300 characters a minute), each error costs roughly two extra keystrokes — and worse, it breaks the rhythm that the next ten words depend on.

That is why a clean 55 WPM run produces more finished text than a sloppy 65 WPM run. Speed and accuracy are not competitors. Accuracy is the mechanism, and speed is what it produces. The typing accuracy test shows both numbers after every round so you can watch the relationship yourself.

## The accuracy-first protocol

For two weeks, type at 90% of your normal speed with one rule: finish every round at 98% accuracy or better, no exceptions. This deliberately sacrifices raw speed, and it feels wrong. What actually happens is your fingers relearn to be right the first time, and within days your error rate drops at every speed.

Then — and only then — start lifting the pace by about 5% a week, holding the 98% line as a hard floor. If accuracy dips below it, drop back. Typists who follow this protocol consistently outpace those who just 'type more', because every week compounds on a clean base.

## Train the two metrics on different days

Once your base is clean, alternate. Speed days: 15-second sprints at maximum effort where errors do not matter — you are stretching the ceiling. Accuracy days: full-minute rounds at controlled pace where 98% is mandatory — you are raising the floor. Your working speed settles between the two, and both move faster together than either does alone.

Watch consistency on the 1 minute typing test result panel. Consistency above 85% — smooth, even keystrokes — is the signature of someone whose speed and accuracy have stopped fighting each other.

## Habits that feed both at once

Eyes on the screen, always: every glance at the keyboard is both an error risk and a speed break. Practice daily for ten minutes rather than weekly for an hour: frequency builds the motor memory both metrics draw on. And drill your two weakest keys every day — they are simultaneously your most likely error and your biggest speed brake.

For structured daily work, the typing tips page collects the highest-leverage habits, and a weekly measured round keeps both trends visible.

## Myths that keep you slow and sloppy

Myth one: 'speed comes from typing fast.' It comes from typing right — the error-cost math makes sloppy speed an illusion. Myth two: 'accuracy and speed are a trade-off.' They are a stack: accuracy is the foundation, speed is what gets built on it. Myth three: 'you are either a fast typist or you are not.' Every elite profile — 98% accuracy, high consistency, symbol fluency — is trained, none is innate.

Myth four is the subtle one: 'slowing down means giving up on speed.' The accuracy-first protocol slows you down for two weeks precisely so that every week after is faster. Typists who skip this step spend years reinforcing the errors that cap them.

## A two-week dual-metric starter plan

Days one to three: baseline week. One measured minute daily at 90% of your normal pace, hard floor of 98% accuracy. Record net WPM, raw WPM and the gap between them. Days four to seven: weak-key elimination — the two worst keys from your report, drilled in 15-second bursts daily, accuracy floor unchanged.

Week two: alternate days. Odd days are speed days — two 15-second sprints at full effort, then a measured minute at normal pace. Even days are accuracy days — controlled rounds at the 98% floor. Day fourteen: full retest against baseline. The expected result is a smaller net-raw gap and a higher net score — both metrics moving at once, which is the whole point.

## Beyond prose: the dual method for numbers, code and forms

The error-cost math intensifies outside prose. In numeric data entry, a single transposed digit can invalidate a whole record, and KPH-quoted tests discard accuracy below a hard cutoff — often 98%. Here the dual protocol is not optional; it is the entire job description. Accuracy days in numbers mode, speed days in short digit sprints, and the same weekly measured minute.

Code and form-filling add another layer: symbols carry meaning, so a misplaced bracket is not a typo but a bug. The same alternation works — accuracy-focused rounds on symbol-heavy text, sprints separately. Whatever the text type, the rule survives: clean first, fast second, both measured weekly.

## Troubleshooting the dual method

If accuracy will not rise past 96%: you are practising at speeds where errors are automatic. Drop to 80% of comfortable pace for a week — boring, but it re-patterns the error itself. If speed stalls while accuracy is high: the ceiling is rhythm, not correctness. Add 15-second sprints daily and let the floor follow later.

If the net-raw gap swings wildly between rounds: inconsistency is the issue, usually from fatigue or posture drift mid-round. Shorter, fresher sessions fix it. And if nothing moves for three weeks: the method is fine — the measurement is stale. Change text type for a week and remeasure; new stimulus often unsticks a stale trend.

## How to Type Faster and More Accurately FAQ

### Can you improve speed and accuracy at the same time?

Yes — they are the same goal once you count error costs. Every error costs about two correction keystrokes, so a clean 55 WPM produces more finished text than a sloppy 65 WPM.

### Should I slow down to improve accuracy?

Temporarily, yes. Two weeks at 90% of your normal speed with a hard 98% accuracy floor re-trains your fingers to be right the first time — then lift the pace 5% a week.

### What accuracy should I aim for?

97-98% minimum before pushing speed. Below that, error correction eats more time than raw speed returns. At elite level, 98%+ is the norm.

### How do I track both metrics?

Take a 1 minute typing test round — the result panel shows net WPM, raw WPM, accuracy and consistency together. Net-minus-raw gap tells you whether to push speed or tighten accuracy.
