# German Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/german-typing-test.html

Real German sentences with the characters that actually slow typists down: ä, ö, ü, ß and the comma-heavy clause structure. Works on a QWERTZ keyboard, no sign up, results stay in your browser.

German adds four characters that English does not use: ä, ö, ü and ß. On a QWERTZ keyboard they sit on dedicated keys to the right of P and L, so they need a reach rather than a modifier - which means they interrupt the hand position less than a Shift combination, but they are pressed less often and therefore practised less.

The second difference is length. German compounds produce long words (Geschwindigkeitsmessung, Tastaturbelegung), and long words mean more consecutive keystrokes per word with no space to reset the hand. Typists who are comfortable at 60 WPM on English often measure 50-55 on German text at the same accuracy.

German-language typing tests usually report two figures. Wörter pro Minute (WPM) counts five characters as one word, the same convention used in English. Anschläge pro Minute (CPM, sometimes Zeichen pro Minute) counts every character, which is what German office job adverts often quote.

Anschläge/Min | Wörter/Min | Einordnung
150 | 30 | Anfangsniveau, Übung nötig
200 | 40 | Solide für Büroarbeit
250 | 50 | Gut, über dem Durchschnitt
300 | 60 | Schnell, für Sekretariat und Datenerfassung
400 | 80 | Sehr schnell, obere 10%

The conversion is arithmetic: WPM = Anschläge ÷ 5. Nothing about German text changes the formula, only the character mix.

German-speaking countries use QWERTZ: Y and Z are swapped relative to QWERTY, and the umlauts sit on their own keys. If you type on a QWERTZ keyboard, this test works as-is, because the browser reports the character your operating system produces, not the physical key.

If you are learning German on a QWERTY keyboard, enable the German layout in your operating system before practising. Typing German text on an English layout means hunting for the umlauts, which trains the wrong habit and produces a score that does not transfer.

Start with one-minute rounds on sentences, then move to five minutes once accuracy holds above 97%. German rewards rhythm more than speed: the comma clauses give you natural places to breathe, and typists who rush through them accumulate errors on the endings.

If your goal is a specific job, check what the advert quotes. Office and Verwaltung roles typically ask 200-300 Anschläge pro Minute; Datenerfassung is quoted in keystrokes per hour, which you can convert on the WPM to KPH converter.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good German typing speed?

200-250 Anschläge pro Minute (40-50 Wörter pro Minute) is solid for office work. Office and administrative roles commonly ask 200-300, and data entry is quoted in keystrokes per hour.

### Does this test work on a QWERTZ keyboard?

Yes. The test compares the characters your operating system produces, so a German layout with umlauts and ß on their own keys works without configuration.

### Why is my German speed lower than my English speed?

German compounds produce longer words with more consecutive keystrokes, and the umlauts are pressed less often than letters, so they stay less practised. A 10% drop at equal accuracy is normal.

### How do I type ä ö ü ß?

On a QWERTZ keyboard they have their own keys to the right of P and L. On a QWERTY keyboard, add the German layout in your OS settings rather than using Alt codes, which do not train the correct motion.

### How many WPM is 200 Anschläge pro Minute?

Forty words per minute. Anschläge ÷ 5 = Wörter pro Minute, the same five-characters-per-word convention used by English typing tests.
