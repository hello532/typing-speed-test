# Japanese Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/japanese-typing-test.html

Test your Japanese typing speed with real Japanese sentences mixing kanji, hiragana and katakana. Instant speed and accuracy results. Free, no sign up.

You type romaji, the input method converts it to kana, and a second conversion turns kana into kanji. So a single word can require typing, converting, and choosing from a candidate list - three decisions per word where English requires none.

That is why Japanese typing speed depends heavily on conversion accuracy rather than raw keystrokes. A typist who converts correctly on the first try is faster than one who types faster but re-converts twice.

Romaji input maps Latin letters to kana (ka → か) and is what most learners use, because it works on any keyboard. Kana input maps each kana to a single key on a Japanese keyboard; it has a higher ceiling because each kana is one keystroke instead of two or three.

For most people romaji is the right choice: the keyboard is standard, the learning curve is shorter, and the ceiling is high enough for office work. Switch to kana input only if you type Japanese for hours a day.

Kanji selection - homophones are common (こうしょう has dozens of kanji readings), so candidate lists matter.
Katakana words - loanwords are long and the conversion is rarely the first candidate.
Names - place and person names often need manual conversion.
Punctuation - the Japanese comma 、 and full stop 。 are separate keys from their Latin counterparts.

This page reports WPM and CPM from the committed text. For Japanese, characters per minute is the meaningful figure, and it is typically much lower than an English WPM for the same typist - a reflection of the conversion work, not of slower fingers.

## Japanese typing test FAQ

### How does Japanese typing work?

You type romaji, the input method converts it to kana, then a second conversion selects kanji from a candidate list. Each word can involve typing, converting and choosing.

### Is romaji or kana input faster?

Kana input has a higher ceiling because each kana is one keystroke, but it requires a Japanese keyboard or layout and a longer learning curve. Romaji is the practical choice for most people.

### Why is my Japanese WPM so low?

Conversion decisions cost time that keystroke speed cannot recover. Japanese characters per minute are typically far below an equivalent English WPM - compare within Japanese over time.

### What are the hardest parts?

Kanji selection for homophones, katakana loanwords, and names. All three require candidate-list navigation rather than faster typing.
