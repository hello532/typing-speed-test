# How Many Words Per Minute Is Fast?

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/how-many-words-per-minute-is-fast.html

How many words per minute is fast? The honest answer depends on what you are measuring for — but the benchmark bands are well established. Here is where your score actually stands.

## The standard WPM benchmark bands

Typing speed falls into widely used bands. Around 40 WPM is the average adult — enough for everyday email and documents. Between 60 and 80 WPM beats most office workers and is the usual 'fast typist' claim on a résumé. Above 80 WPM is professional territory, and anything past 100 WPM puts you in roughly the top one percent of typists.

WPM band | Level | What it means in practice
20–30 | Beginner | Hunt-and-peck or early touch typing
40–50 | Average adult | Everyday computer work, no formal training
60–80 | Good / office standard | Comfortable for any office job
80–100 | Fast (top ~10%) | Professional-level speed
100+ | Elite (top ~1%) | Competitive typists, years of practice

## Fast for what? Context decides

For everyday office work, 50–60 WPM with high accuracy is genuinely fast — nobody reads your emails any quicker. For a résumé claim, 60 WPM is the minimum that impresses, and 75+ is strong. For data entry and transcription, speed is quoted in CPM or KPH instead: 300 CPM (60 WPM) is the usual professional floor. The typing speed requirements table breaks these down by job and exam.

For students, anything above 40 WPM is fine, because coursework rewards thought per minute, not keystrokes. And for competitive typing, 'fast' starts around 120 WPM — a different sport entirely.

## Accuracy changes what 'fast' means

A 70 WPM run at 92% accuracy is slower in real work than a 55 WPM run at 99%, because every error costs the character plus about two correction keystrokes. That is why employers and this site quote net WPM — speed with errors deducted. If your accuracy is below 97%, your fastest path to a higher 'fast' is cleaning errors, not pushing raw speed.

## How to measure your own WPM properly

Take a words per minute test three times and use the middle score — single runs vary by several points. Test at your normal working time of day, on your normal keyboard, and compare net WPM, not raw. ## How WPM translates across languages and scripts

The 40/60/80/100 bands are English benchmarks, and they travel imperfectly. Japanese and Chinese pack meaning into single characters, so a 'word' carries more content and raw WPM reads lower for the same real output. Agglutinative languages like Turkish stretch single words into long compounds, which pushes WPM down even at high keystroke speeds.

For cross-language comparison, CPM — characters per minute — is the honest metric, because a keystroke is a keystroke in every script. The five-characters-per-word standard converts cleanly: 200 CPM is the 40 WPM average, 300 CPM the office-standard 60.

## The WPM you need for school and exams

For schoolwork, 30–40 WPM is plenty: essays are limited by thinking speed, not finger speed, and anything above that range simply sits unused. Timed exams that include a typing component — clerk, banking and government tests in several countries — typically qualify at 25–35 WPM with an accuracy cap, a bar most regular computer users already clear.

Where speed becomes a differentiator is in timed note-taking, live transcription and chat-based customer work, all of which reward the 50–65 WPM range. Beyond that, the returns shrink fast: the difference between 80 and 100 WPM matters to almost no employer.

## How long it takes to move between bands

Moving from 20 to 40 WPM is the quickest band jump — four to eight weeks of daily home-row practice, because you are replacing hunt-and-peck with a system. From 40 to 60 takes a few months of accuracy-first work; you are now refining technique, not learning one. From 60 to 80 typically takes a year of deliberate practice, and 80 to 100 takes one to two more years of structured training.

The pattern to notice: each band takes longer than the last, and each is broken by a technique change rather than more volume. Plateaus are normal at 55–65 and again at 75–85 — they are signals to change drills, not to type more.

## How employers actually test WPM

When a company measures your typing, the format is almost always the same: a one- to three-minute passage, typed once or twice, scored as net WPM with an accuracy threshold — often 95% or 98% — below which the speed number is discarded. Nobody averages your best days; they sample your ordinary ones.

That has two practical consequences. First, your 'interview number' is your relaxed, warmed-down score, not your personal best, so practise the conditions: unfamiliar text, no warm-up beyond a minute, one take. Second, accuracy is a gate, not a bonus — a 70 WPM run at 93% loses to a 55 WPM run at 99% in any scored test. Train the floor, and the number takes care of itself.

## WPM on phones vs physical keyboards

Everything on this page refers to physical keyboards. Phone typing runs on different mechanics — two thumbs, autocorrect, predictive text — and good mobile typists reach 35–50 WPM equivalent, with the very fastest measured higher under test conditions. Comparing phone WPM to keyboard WPM is meaningless because the error profile differs: autocorrect silently fixes some mistakes and invents others.

If most of your writing happens on a phone, train that separately. If it happens on a computer, the keyboard bands above are your reference. The crossover skill that helps both is the same one: accuracy first, because corrections are the most expensive keystrokes on any device.

## How Many Words Per Minute Is Fast? FAQ

### Is 60 words per minute fast?

Yes for general use — 60 WPM beats the 40 WPM adult average and meets the office standard for almost any job. Above 80 WPM is where 'fast' becomes genuinely rare.

### Is 100 words per minute possible?

Yes, but it is elite territory — roughly the top 1% of typists, usually after years of deliberate practice with near-perfect accuracy.

### What is a good WPM for a job?

Most office jobs are comfortable at 40–60 WPM. Data entry and transcription roles quote CPM or KPH instead — 300 CPM (60 WPM) is the common professional floor.

### How do I check my WPM?

Take three rounds of a free words per minute test and use the middle net score. Test on your usual keyboard at your usual time of day for a comparable result.
