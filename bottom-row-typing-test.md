# Bottom Row Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/bottom-row-typing-test.html

Drill the bottom row &mdash; Z X C V B N M. Seven keys, one of them the rarest letter in English, and the reach that costs most people the most accuracy. Type the passage and watch where your errors land.

The bottom row is Z X C V B N M, the row below the home keys. The left hand owns Z, X, C, V and B; the right hand owns N and M. It is the shortest letter row, with seven keys against ten on the top row.

The row split is the part people get wrong. B belongs to the left index finger and N belongs to the right index finger, which means the middle of this row is the same left/right boundary as the top row's T and Y.

Every bottom-row keystroke is a reach away from home and a return, so a sloppy return throws off the letter after it as well. In longer words that compounds: two or three letters typed from a shifted hand position is what a typo actually looks like.

The row also pairs the rarest letter in English, Z, with frequent ones like N and C. Because Z comes up so rarely in normal text, it never gets practised by accident, which is exactly why drilling the row deliberately helps.

Keep the finger guide on and type slowly enough that each return to home is clean. Accuracy first: a round at 40 WPM with 98 percent accuracy trains the right movement, while 70 WPM at 88 percent trains the mistake.

Once the row feels automatic, compare it against your home row score. A large gap between the two usually means this row, not the home row, is what holds your real-text speed back.

## Bottom row FAQ

### What are the bottom row keys?

Z, X, C, V, B, N, M. The left hand types Z X C V B and the right hand types N and M.

### Which finger types B and N?

B is typed with the left index finger and N with the right index finger, so the split sits between B and N.

### Why is the bottom row harder than it looks?

It only has seven keys, but every one is a reach down from home, and it mixes the rarest English letter, Z, with common ones like N and C. Rare keys never get practised by accident.

### Should I learn the bottom row last?

Yes. Home row first, then the top row, then this one. Each row only makes sense once the return-to-home movement is automatic.

### What is a good bottom row accuracy?

Aim for 97 percent or better at a slow speed before raising it. Below about 95 percent the errors are usually hand position, not finger speed.
