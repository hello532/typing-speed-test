## Arabic typing test

An Arabic typing test measures how fast and accurately you type Arabic script. Your result is shown in words per minute, where every 5 characters counts as one word. Typing full Arabic sentences — with connected letters, the ة ta marbuta and the أ hamza — is the most realistic way to measure everyday Arabic typing speed.

## How to improve Arabic typing

Practise with real sentences rather than isolated letters, keep your fingers on the home row, and watch the screen instead of the keyboard. Arabic is typed right to left, so let your hands move with the text from right to left; slow down at first and let speed follow.

## Right-to-left typing

Arabic script runs right to left, and most letters connect to what follows them, changing shape by position. Typing real sentences trains the frequent letters — ا ل م و ي — and the word-final letter forms that appear in almost every Arabic sentence.

## Practising Arabic typing

This Arabic typing test uses natural, everyday sentences, so you practise the real word patterns of Arabic — the ال prefix and endings like ـها and ـكم — together with the ، and . punctuation you meet in real Arabic writing.

## Arabic typing test FAQ

### Is this Arabic typing test free?

Yes. The Arabic typing test is free with no sign up; your right-to-left practice history stays on your device.

### Does the test type right to left?

Yes. The whole page switches to right-to-left direction for Arabic, so letters join and flow exactly as they do in Arabic text.

### Does the test include hamza and ta marbuta?

Yes. The practice sentences are real Arabic, so they include the أ إ آ ؤ ئ hamza forms, the ة ta marbuta, and ؛ ، punctuation.

### How is WPM counted for Arabic text?

The standard 5-characters-per-word rule, adjusted for errors. Joined letter forms still count per typed character, so smooth right-to-left flow beats bursts.
