# Typing Test for Virtual Assistants

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/typing-test-for-virtual-assistant.html

Virtual assistant work is mostly written: inboxes, calendars, CRM notes, research summaries. Clients commonly look for 55–65 WPM with clean accuracy, and many screen with a paid trial task rather than a typing test.

## Why the bar sits at 55-65 WPM

A VA's output is measured in completed tasks, and most tasks are typed: replies, notes, data cleanup, scheduling messages. At 45 WPM a morning of inbox work takes three hours; at 65 WPM it takes two, which is the entire margin a client is paying for.

That is why VA postings cluster at 50-65 WPM rather than the 40 WPM typical of in-office clerical roles. The work is not harder, it is simply a bigger share of the job.

## What clients actually check

Clean written English. Typos in a client-facing email cost more than a slow reply. Accuracy is the first filter.
Formatting discipline. Bullets, dates, time zones, signatures - the same output every time.
Tool fluency. Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, a CRM, a scheduling tool, and increasingly an AI assistant used to draft rather than to replace judgement.
Turnaround. A trial task with a deadline is the most common screen, and it tests all four at once.

## How to prove your speed remotely

Keep a dated five-minute result with a visible WPM and accuracy, and mention the test length. "62 WPM net, 99% accuracy, five-minute test" is credible; "fast typist" is not.

If a client asks for a trial, deliver it in the format you would actually use - correct file naming, a short cover note, no stray formatting from a copy-paste. Half of VA trials fail on presentation rather than content.

## The one skill worth adding

Dictation and editing. Drafting by voice and cleaning up by keyboard is how experienced VAs double throughput on long-form work, and it is a different skill from typing tests: you need fast, accurate editing rather than fast transcription.

Practise by dictating a paragraph, then editing it to publishable quality in under two minutes. If the edited version reads better than your typed draft, you have the workflow.

## Frequently asked questions

### What typing speed do virtual assistants need?

Most postings ask for 50-65 WPM with high accuracy. The upper end reflects that nearly all VA output is typed, so speed converts directly into completed tasks.

### Do clients test typing speed?

Some ask for a score, but many use a paid trial task instead. A trial tests speed, accuracy, formatting and reliability at the same time, which a typing test cannot.

### Is 50 WPM enough to start?

Yes for general admin support, provided your written accuracy is high. Specialist work such as transcription, bookkeeping support or medical admin usually expects 60 WPM or more.

### How do I prove my speed without a proctored test?

Keep a dated five-minute result showing WPM and accuracy, and offer to re-test live on a call. That is usually accepted because it is verifiable in the moment.

### Does dictation experience help?

Yes. Drafting by voice and editing by keyboard is faster than typing long documents, and it is a selling point for clients with heavy written output.
