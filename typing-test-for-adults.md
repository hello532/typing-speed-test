The average adult typing speed is about 40 WPM. Reaching 60 WPM puts you comfortably above average and is enough for most office work; 80 WPM is fast, and 100 WPM+ is top-tier. Age or when you learned to type matter far less than how much you practise.

It is a common myth that only children can build typing speed. Adults usually improve faster than children because they follow instructions, understand why accuracy comes before speed and can practise deliberately. The catch is unlearning years of two-finger habit: the fix is to slow down, keep the fingers on the home row, and look at the screen instead of the keyboard.

Ten to fifteen minutes a day is enough. Start with the home row typing test, then the touch typing test, and only push for raw speed once your accuracy is steady.

Decide what you need. For a job application, run a 1-3 minute test and check the requirement table in the typing test for jobs. For everyday comfort, test once a day at 1 minute and watch the trend in your saved history. For data entry or accounting work, add the 10 key typing test. Almost every adult who types slowly learned by using a computer, not by being taught. That means the habits formed were whatever got the work done: two fingers, looking at the keyboard, backspacing through errors. None of those are permanent, but all of them are invisible until you try to type without looking.

The encouraging part is that adults learn the key map faster than children do - you already read and write fluently, so the bottleneck is motor habit rather than language. Two weeks of covered rounds typically produces the first real jump.

A job requirement - clerical, reception, dispatch, legal and medical support postings quote 40-60 WPM and often test it.
A return to work - the test is usually a five-minute sample with an accuracy floor, not a race.
Career change into admin or support - the typing bar is modest but the accuracy expectation is not.
Writing volume - reports, emails and documentation that used to be someone else's job.
Comfort - less hunting means less wrist and shoulder tension by the end of a day.

Week 1 is slower and frustrating: you are replacing a working habit. Weeks 2-3 bring accuracy up and speed back to where it started. Week 4 usually produces the first measurable gain, often 5-8 WPM, mostly from no longer looking down.

If you are over 50, add one thing: a five-minute break every half hour during practice, and stop at the first sign of hand or wrist discomfort. The skill builds fine at that pace; strain does not.

## Adult typing test FAQ

### What is a good typing speed for an adult?

Around 40 WPM is average for adults, 60 to 80 WPM is good and enough for most jobs, and 100 WPM or more is elite. Consistent practice matters more than age.

### Can adults learn to type faster?

Yes. Adults often improve faster than children because they can practise deliberately. Focus on accuracy and touch typing rather than raw speed at first.

### How much practice do adults need?

Ten to fifteen minutes a day is enough to see steady improvement within a few weeks, as long as you keep your eyes off the keyboard.

### Do adults need to learn touch typing?

Not everyone, but touch typing removes the speed ceiling of two-finger typing and reduces strain during long sessions, so it pays off for anyone who types daily.

### Is this typing test free for adults?

Yes, completely free, with no sign-up and no ads in the typing area. Your results are saved only in your own browser.

### Can adults learn to touch type?

Yes, and often faster than children on the key map, because reading and writing are already fluent. The slower part is replacing the two-finger habit, which takes about two weeks of short daily covered practice.

### What typing speed do adult job seekers need?

40-50 WPM with 97% accuracy covers most clerical, reception and admin postings. Legal, medical and transcription roles ask 60-75, and public-sector tests often require a proctored certificate.
