# 40 WPM Typing Test

Source: https://typing.rerivo.com/40-wpm-typing-test.html

Forty words per minute is the number that appears in more job postings than any other: 40 WPM, five minutes, few errors. It is roughly the 28th percentile — the threshold that separates 'can type' from 'can be employed to type'.

40 WPM is 200 characters per minute, 12,000 keystrokes per hour, and 200 words over a five-minute test. On a standard error rule of one error per ten words, you are allowed about twenty errors across the five minutes — which sounds generous until you watch your score after a bad paragraph.

The US federal clerical standard is published as 40 WPM over five minutes with no more than three errors, which is far stricter: 98.5% accuracy. Most private employers quote the speed and leave the accuracy floor at 95%.

Role | Stated requirement | At 40 WPM
Receptionist | 40-50 net WPM | Bottom of the band
General office / admin | 40-55 WPM | Bottom of the band
County clerk-typist | 35-45 net WPM | Comfortable
US federal clerk | 40 WPM, ≤3 errors | Exactly at the line
Data entry clerk | 45-65 WPM or 13,500+ KPH | Short

Being exactly at the line is risky on a five-minute test, because one bad sentence can drop you under it. Aim for 44-45 before you apply, which gives you the margin that 40 alone does not.

Week 1 — measure and stop the bleeding. Three five-minute rounds, no backspace rule, accuracy recorded. Most candidates at 40 WPM are correcting 8-12 times a minute; removing that habit alone buys 3-5 WPM.

Week 2 — cover the keyboard. One round a day with hands covered. This is uncomfortable for three days and then stops being an issue, and it is the difference between a ceiling at 45 and no ceiling.

Week 3 — length and mode. Five-minute sentence rounds three times a week, one numbers round a week. Add capitals and punctuation deliberately: real tests include them and untrained typists drop them under time pressure.

Week 4 — simulate. Full-length, time of day matching your interview, unfamiliar keyboard. Then test once and use that number on applications.

Stop training for 40 and move to the 60 WPM plan; the skill work is the same but the drills change. At 55 you are already above the requirement for almost every clerical role, so further gains are for transcription, legal and medical work rather than for clearing a gate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 40 WPM good enough for a job?

For most clerical, receptionist and county government roles, yes — 40 WPM is the figure those postings quote. Aim for 44-45 so a bad sentence on a five-minute test cannot drop you below the line.

### What percentile is 40 WPM?

About the 28th percentile of the CHI 2018 sample (mean 51.56 WPM, standard deviation 20.2). Roughly seven in ten measured typists are faster.

### How many keystrokes per hour is 40 WPM?

12,000 KPH. Forty words per minute is 200 characters per minute, and 200 × 60 = 12,000.

### How long is the federal 40 WPM test?

Five minutes, with no more than three errors in most published announcements. That is 98.5% accuracy, stricter than the 95% floor private employers usually apply.

### How fast can I go from 30 to 40 WPM?

Two to six weeks of daily ten-minute practice for most people, mostly from removing the look-at-keyboard habit and reducing corrections rather than from raw speed drills.
