## English typing test

An English typing test measures how fast and accurately you type English text. Your result is shown in WPM (words per minute), where every 5 characters counts as one word. Typing full English sentences — with punctuation and capitals — is the most realistic way to measure everyday typing speed.

## How to improve English typing

Practise with real sentences rather than isolated words, keep your fingers on the home row (ASDF JKL;), and look at the screen instead of the keyboard. Accuracy first, then speed: 10-15 focused minutes a day beats long occasional sessions.

## Practising English typing

This English typing test uses natural, everyday sentences rather than random letters, so you practise the real word patterns and spacing you meet in emails, essays and chat. Typing whole phrases trains the common letter pairs — "th", "ing", "and", "tion" — that make fluent English typing feel effortless.

## Why common words speed you up

A small set of words makes up most of what we write in English. Drilling high-frequency words like the, and, that, with and your builds instant muscle memory for the keystrokes you repeat thousands of times a day. Master those and your overall WPM rises across every kind of text you type.

## Typing test FAQ

### Is this English typing test free?

Yes. The English typing test is free with no sign up, and your English WPM history is saved only in this browser.

### What is a good English typing speed?

40 WPM is average, 60-70 WPM is good, and 80+ WPM is professional level for most office and writing work.

### How is English typing speed calculated?

The standard 5-characters-per-word rule, applied to real English sentences. Because English words average close to 5 characters, the formula matches real English text well; errors are subtracted for net WPM.

### Does English word length affect my WPM?

Slightly: WPM counts five characters as one word, and common English words average just under five, so scores on real sentences run a touch higher than on random-word drills. The difference is small — a few WPM — and consistent, so scores compare fairly.
