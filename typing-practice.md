## typing practice

Regular typing practice is the only reliable way to get faster. Ten to fifteen minutes a day beats one long session once a week, because your muscles learn the key positions through repetition. Use this page every day: pick a mode, type the text, then compare your result with yesterday. Try to keep accuracy above 95 percent at all times. This doubles as typing test practice: the same engine, the same scoring, so a drill round here is a rehearsal for the real thing.

## Typing exercises for every skill

Change the text mode to target your weak spots. Sentences build everyday typing fluency. Quotes add capital letters and punctuation. Numbers train the top row, which is the slowest part of the keyboard for most people. Words mode drills common English vocabulary so high-frequency words become automatic.

## A simple practice plan

Week one: two minute sentence tests, focus on accuracy. Week two: add quotes and numbers, keep the same two minutes. Week three: try five minute tests to build endurance. Week four: switch back to one minute and compare your WPM with week one. Most people gain 10 to 20 WPM in a month with this routine. Treat a short round as a typing quiz: ten to twenty seconds, one attempt, score recorded — a quick check-in that tells you whether the week’s practice is actually moving your speed.

## When practice stops working

A plateau near 50 or 70 WPM usually means one bottleneck, not general slowness: check your slowest keys in the result panel after each round. Spend a week drilling only those keys in accuracy test rounds at 97%+ before chasing speed again. If accuracy is fine but speed stalls, your rhythm is uneven — try 2 minute tests at a metronome-steady pace instead of sprinting. Most plateaus break within two focused weeks.

## The four bottlenecks practice must target

Every slow typist is slow for one of four reasons, and each needs a different drill. Accuracy bottlenecks show as a wide gap between net and raw speed — fix them with controlled rounds at a 98% floor. Speed bottlenecks show as clean but slow rounds — fix them with 15-second sprints that stretch the ceiling. Symbol bottlenecks show as collapses on numbers and punctuation — fix them with dedicated numbers and quotes rounds, because everyday prose never trains those keys.

Stamina bottlenecks show as accuracy decaying in the final third of a long test — fix them with weekly three-to-five-minute endurance rounds. Read your result panel in that order and the panel tells you which bottleneck you have this week. Practice without this diagnosis is just repetition.

## Practising for a specific goal

For an employment test: practise the exact format — same duration, unfamiliar text, one take — twice a week, and hold accuracy two points above the gate. For raw speed: alternate sprint days and rhythm days, and accept that gains past 80 WPM come in single points per month. For a new language: practise that language's word pool daily; accuracy habits transfer between scripts faster than vocabulary does.

For school or everyday competence: you need far less than you think. Forty WPM at 97% accuracy covers almost every daily need, and the typing speed requirements table shows what specific jobs actually ask for — most are lower than people fear.

## How to know your practice is working

Weekly, not daily: one measured round under identical conditions, recorded with the date. Three numbers tell you everything — net WPM rising means the system works; accuracy falling means you are pushing too hard; consistency dropping means fatigue or posture drift needs attention before any drill.

Keep the log where you will actually look at it. A simple note with twelve weeks of numbers is more motivating than any badge system, and it is the only reliable way to catch a plateau in week one instead of week six.

## Common practice mistakes

Practising speed first: every error costs about two correction keystrokes, so sloppy speed practice builds a faster way to be slow. Practising rarely: an hour on Sunday consolidates worse than ten minutes daily, because motor memory forms between sessions. Practising without measurement: if you are not tracking a number, you are rehearsing, not improving.

And the quietest one: practising only what you are already good at. Comfortable text feels productive and fixes nothing. The weak-key report is the opposite of comfortable — and that is exactly where the gains live. ## Typing test FAQ

### What is typing practice?

Untimed, repeatable drills across sentences, quotes, numbers and words modes. Unlike a scored test, practice lets you retry weak keys freely and watch per-key accuracy improve before you take a timed test.

### How is my practice speed scored?

Practice rounds use the same formula — correct characters divided by 5 and by minutes spent — plus accuracy and slowest-key stats. Scores save locally so you can compare rounds over time.

### How long should a practice session be?

Ten to fifteen minutes a day beats an occasional hour. Short daily sessions build muscle memory; stop when accuracy drops, since practising mistakes ingrains them.

### Is typing practice free?

Yes, free forever with no account. Every drill, language, theme and duration is unlocked, and nothing uploads to a server.

### How do I type faster?

Practice daily for 10–15 minutes, prioritise accuracy over speed, keep your wrists relaxed and your fingers anchored on the home row (ASDF JKL;). Learn to use all ten fingers rather than hunting and pecking with two.

### Can I use this page for typing test practice?

Yes. Every drill runs on the same engine and scoring as the timed tests, so a practice round rehearses exactly what a typing test measures. Run a short quiz-style round to warm up, then switch to a 1 minute typing test when you want a score to report.
