## The trainer's four week plan

Week one is diagnosis: run three drills a day and read the error report without trying to fix anything. Week two, drill your two weakest keys twice as often as the rest. Week three, run the full minute at a speed where you make no errors, and live there. Week four, chase your personal best once a day and log it. Five words per minute each week is the only target, because tendons keep their own pace.

## Why training beats practice

Practice is typing a lot; training is typing the right thing a lot. The difference is the log. When you record your WPM and accuracy side by side each day, a speed that costs accuracy shows up immediately as a bad trade, and the drill that fixed your weak keys shows up as a permanent step up. Your hands will plateau; the trainer's answer is variety and rest, not more effort.

## Typing trainer FAQ

### Is this typing trainer free?

Yes, completely free, with no sign up and no account. Your best scores are stored only in your own browser.

### What is the difference between a typing trainer and a typing test?

A test measures you once. A trainer gives you drills sized for daily use, keeps your best score so you always have a target, and points your next session at your weakest keys.

### How long should a training drill be?

Sixty seconds per set, with short rest between sets. Keep total training under thirty minutes a day; a tired hand practices its own mistakes.

### How fast can a typing trainer make me?

Most people add five to ten words per minute per month with daily drills. The reliable ceiling for daily practice is around 80 to 100 WPM, and accuracy above 95 percent is what makes that speed stick.

### Should I train when my hands hurt?

No. Stop, rest, and check your posture and wrist position. Rest days are part of the training program, because muscle memory is written during recovery, not during strain.

### How do I know the training is working?

Log your WPM and accuracy side by side each day. Speed that drops accuracy is not progress. The clearest sign is your error report shrinking week over week, because fewer errors always precede faster speed.
